# Georgia (South Caucasus) — Macroeconomic and Business Climate Briefing, 2025–2026

Research date: 29 September 2026. Scope: Georgia the country only. Method note: full-page fetches of most primary sources (imf.org, worldbank.org, nbg.gov.ge, geostat.ge, adb.org, civil.ge, oc-media.org, expathub.ge, wikipedia) were blocked by the network egress proxy in this session, so findings below rest on search-engine result extracts of those pages plus secondary reporting. ## Key Question 1: Headline macro — GDP growth, inflation, GEL, unemployment, wages, remittances, FDI (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Georgia has run one of the fastest growth rates in wider Europe for four years (9.7% in 2024, 7.5% in 2025, ~6.9–8.6% y/y in Q2–June 2026), with 2026 full-year consensus in the 5–6.5% range and convergence to ~5% potential by 2027–28. Inflation re-accelerated above the 3% target in 2026 (5.6% in August 2026) because of Iran-war energy prices; the lari has been strong (USD/GEL ~2.60 in late September 2026 vs a 2025 average of 2.74); unemployment is at a two-decade low (13.3% Q4 2025) and nominal wages are rising ~10% a year (GEL 2,466/month Q4 2025).

### Inferences
- The macro backdrop for a new business is unusually benign for a frontier market: high nominal growth (~12–13% nominal), a firm currency (lower import costs for equipment), falling unemployment and double-digit wage growth (rising consumer purchasing power but also rising labour costs).
- Growth quality is a concern: FDI is 82% reinvested earnings with little new equity, remittances are shifting from Russia to the EU/US diaspora, and the current account is set to widen in 2026. Consumer-facing and services businesses are riding domestic demand more than export dynamism.
- The 2026 inflation overshoot is supply-driven (fuel); NBG is unlikely to cut soon, so GEL borrowing costs stay high through at least early 2027.

## Key Question 2: Which sectors are growing fastest and which are shrinking?

### Takeaway
ICT, transport/logistics (Middle Corridor), tourism receipts, finance and real-estate services are the growth leaders in 2025–26; construction permits are falling sharply (a pipeline peak in 2026 followed by a supply drop), the energy sector has seen FDI collapse, and tourism arrivals were dented by the Iran war in March–April 2026 even as revenue records were set in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- ICT: fastest-growing sector since 2022; 8.2% of nominal GDP in 9M25; IT turnover GEL 4.5 bn in 9M25 (+2.1x y/y); ICT exports USD 842 m in 2024 (10.9% of services exports) and USD 898 m in 9M25 (+52.3% y/y, 14% of services exports) after a −9.2% decline in 2024; sector grew ~29% in 2025 — [Galt & Taggart IT Sector report, Dec 2025](https://ramad.bog.ge/galt/IT-sector-in-Georgia.pdf); [GBC](https://www.gbc.ge/en/news/Economics-news/ict-exports-surge-523-year-on-year-in-3q25-galt-taggart); [OC Media](https://oc-media.org/georgias-2025-gdp-grows-by-7-5-as-it-sector-soars/)
- IT sector composition: 72 companies classified large/medium; the bulk of the rest are foreign sole proprietors (1% regime) — [World Finance](https://www.worldfinance.com/markets/georgia-rises-as-a-tech-hotspot-but-for-how-long)
- Tourism 2025: 7.8–8.2 million international visitors (sources differ on visitor vs trip count); revenue USD 4.3 bn (one source) or USD 4.69 bn (+6%, another source); Q3 2025 receipts USD 1.66 bn record; Russia largest source market in Q3 2025 with 567,100 visitors (26.1%); EU visitors +26% y/y — [Caucasus Business Journal](https://caucasusbusinessjournal.com/news/georgia-tourism-8-2-million-visitors); [InternationalInvestment.biz](https://internationalinvestment.biz/en/tourism/6940-record-tourism-revenues-in-georgia-2025-results.html); [Georgia Today Q3](https://georgiatoday.ge/georgia-experiences-7-growth-in-international-visitors-in-q3-2025/)

### Inferences
- Transport/logistics (+19.8% Q2 2026) and ICT (+16.6%) are the structural winners; leisure/recreation (+17.4%) suggests domestic and tourist discretionary spending is strong despite the arrivals dip.
- Residential construction is entering a supply cliff (permits −34% YTD 2026) after a 2026 completions peak — negative for construction-input suppliers from 2027, but supportive for rents and for fit-out/facility/operator businesses in newly delivered stock.
- Tourism is geopolitically exposed twice over: Russia is the largest source market (sanctions/politics risk) and the Iran war cut regional air travel in 2026.

## Key Question 3: Special tax regimes for small businesses and founders, and 2025–2026 changes

### Takeaway
Georgia's low-tax menu remains intact in 2026: 1% turnover tax for Individual Entrepreneurs with Small Business Status up to GEL 500k (3% above), 0% Micro Business up to GEL 30k, Estonian-model 15% CIT paid only on distribution plus 5% dividend WHT, 20% flat PIT, 18% VAT with GEL 100k registration threshold, Virtual Zone (0% CIT on foreign IT income), International Company (5% CIT, 0% dividends), Free Industrial Zones, and a new Innovative Startup status (from 24 Sep 2025) with 0% payroll PIT for 3 years. February 2026 amendments tightened SBS administration (status effective from application date, mandatory monthly filing even at zero income, no same-year reinstatement).

### Cited Findings
- Standard rates 2026: PIT 20% flat; CIT 15% on distributed profit (Estonian model, no tax on retained/reinvested profit); dividend WHT 5%; VAT 18% with mandatory registration above GEL 100,000 turnover in any 12 months; pension "2+2+2" (employee 2%, employer 2%, state 2%) — [Legalese Georgia](https://legalese.ge/blog/taxes-in-georgia-guide/); [IBCCS](https://ibccs.tax/blog/georgia-corporate-tax/); [Legal.ge](https://legal.ge/en/service/tax-law-en/georgia-corporate-income-tax-15-estonian-model)
- Small Business Status (SBS): 1% on turnover up to GEL 500,000/year; 3% from the month the threshold is exceeded for the rest of the year; excluded activities include consulting of any kind, legal/notarial, tax/audit, medical, architectural, financial intermediation/currency exchange, gambling, excise goods, staffing — [Andersen Georgia](https://ge.andersen.com/1-tax-regime/); [Gegidze prohibited list](https://www.gegidze.com/post/the-full-list-of-prohibited-activities-that-nullify-georgia-s-small-business-status); [ExpatHub](https://expathub.ge/tax-freelancers-individuals-small-businesses-georgia/)
- Micro Business: 0% tax up to GEL 30,000/year, no employees allowed; on exceeding GEL 30k or hiring, if SBS is applied for within 15 calendar days the switch is seamless from that date (2026 rule) — [ExpatHub 2026 update](https://expathub.ge/2026-updates-to-the-special-tax-regimes/); [Eurofast Feb 2026](https://eurofast.eu/georgia-amends-special-tax-regimes-for-micro-and-small-businesses-february-2026-update/)
- 2026 SBS amendments: (1) status effective from date of application, not the following month; (2) failure to file a monthly return is now a tax offence with a fine even at zero income (no longer treated as a zero return); (3) after cancellation, re-application only from the next tax year — [ExpatHub](https://expathub.ge/2026-updates-to-the-special-tax-regimes/); [IBCCS 2026](https://ibccs.tax/blog/georgia-1-tax-regime-in-2026-individual-entrepreneur-and-small-business-status/); [Gegidze](https://www.gegidze.com/post/2026-special-tax-regimes-sbs-1-tax-micro-business-and-more)
- Virtual Zone Person (VZP): 0% CIT on income from IT services supplied to foreign clients; 5% dividend WHT still applies; other taxes as normal; cannot be held simultaneously with International Company status (IC status auto-revoked if VZP granted) — [Andersen VZP](https://ge.andersen.com/virtual-zone-person-georgia/); [Forbes Georgia 2025 guide](https://forbes.ge/en/virtual-zone-person-vs-international-it-company-in-georgia-2025-tax-guide-for-it-businesses/)
- International Company (IC) status (IT and maritime): 5% CIT on distributed profit from eligible activity; 0% dividend WHT; reduced PIT on salaries (5% per Andersen guide) — [Andersen IC](https://ge.andersen.com/international-company-status-georgia/); [Forbes Georgia](https://forbes.ge/en/virtual-zone-person-vs-international-it-company-in-georgia-2025-tax-guide-for-it-businesses/)
- Government Resolutions N420 and N422 of 24 Sep 2025 amended the VZP (Res. 49) and IC (Res. 619) rules to govern transition to the new "innovation permit"/Innovative Startup status — [Kreston Georgia, Sep 2025](https://kreston.ge/en/finansuri-blogi/blogi/2025-%E1%83%AC%E1%83%9A%E1%83%98%E1%83%A1-%E1%83%A1%E1%83%94%E1%83%A5%E1%83%A2%E1%83%94%E1%83%9B%E1%83%91%E1%83%A0%E1%83%98%E1%83%A1-%E1%83%97%E1%83%95%E1%83%98%E1%83%A1-%E1%83%92%E1%83%90%E1%83%9C/)
- Innovative Startup status (Law on Innovations amendments June 2025, effective 24 Sep 2025): three statuses — Innovative Startup, Innovative SME, R&D Service Provider. Innovative Startup gets 0% PIT on salaries years 1–3, 5% years 4–6, 10% years 7–10; CIT 0%/5%/10% on the same schedule; continuation into years 4–6 requires ~GEL 5 m cumulative investment; eligibility requires prior investment ≥ GEL 100,000 from a fund/angel network/accelerator or ≥ GEL 150,000 GITA grant; GITA reviews in 10 calendar days; GEL 200 fee — [ExpatHub startup guide](https://expathub.ge/georgia-startup-tax-benefits-2026/); [Andersen](https://ge.andersen.com/georgia-innovation-tax-incentives/); [LTA](https://lta.ge/en/article/amendments-to-the-law-of-georgia-on-innovations-2025-new-definitions-startup-status-and-tax-incentives); [Commersant](https://commersant.ge/en/news/business/the-law-on-tax-incentives-for-innovative-startups-in-georgia-has-come-into-effect-today)
- Free Industrial Zones: four operational FIZs in Poti (est. 2008, 300 ha), Tbilisi (est. 2015, ~17 ha, ~150 companies), Kutaisi (Hualing) and another Kutaisi zone; 0% CIT/VAT/property tax on export activity; 4% tax on revenue from sales into Georgia (plus VAT/import duties); PIT on employees still applies — [ExpatHub FIZ](https://expathub.ge/0-tax-with-conditions-free-industrial-zones-of-georgia/); [Healy Consultants](https://www.healyconsultants.com/georgia-company-registration/free-zones/); [PB Services](https://pbservices.ge/blog/free-zones-in-georgia-difference/)
- Gambling: 2026 bill for international-operator licences serving only non-residents at 5% GGR tax (vs 20% domestic); fines for permit violations raised from 1 June 2026 (online casinos GEL 7,000→20,000; slot halls GEL 1,000→10,000) — [Parameter.io](https://parameter.io/georgia-proposes-special-gambling-licenses-for-international-operators-in-2026/); [GamblingMaps](https://gamblingmaps.org/map/regulations/georgia)

### Inferences
- For a founder testing a services idea, the cheapest structure is an Individual Entrepreneur with SBS (1%), provided the activity is not on the excluded list (consulting is excluded — a common trap). For a product/tech company expecting to raise capital, an LLC that later applies for Innovative Startup status is the new optimal path, superseding Virtual Zone for many.
- The 2026 SBS tightening signals the Revenue Service is policing the 1% regime more actively; dormant IE registrations now carry fine risk.
- The Estonian CIT model makes reinvestment tax-free, so capital-intensive businesses (facilities, equipment) benefit disproportionately.

- Whether the international gambling licence bill was enacted (as of Sept 2026) is unconfirmed.
- Property tax rates (up to 1% on business property) not verified in this session.

## Key Question 4: Ease of registering, banking, hiring, licensing — common frictions

### Takeaway
Company registration remains one of the world's fastest (GEL 100, one business day, at the Public Service Hall/House of Justice), but the friction has moved to banking (tightened FATF-driven KYC with real refusal risk for non-residents) and to immigration: from 1 March 2026 Georgia introduced a mandatory work-permit regime for foreigners actively working in Georgia and raised residence-by-property to USD 150,000.

### Cited Findings
- LLC registration: state fee GEL 100 (standard, one business day) or GEL 200 (expedited/same day); registration at House of Justice offices (Tbilisi, Batumi, Rustavi, etc.) — [Georgia Expats](https://georgiaexpats.com/guides/llc-registration-georgia/); [Tbilisi Expat](https://tbilisi-expat.com/blog/open-llc-georgia); [Gegidze](https://www.gegidze.com/post/georgia-llc-registration-cost-government-fees-professional-s)
- Bank accounts: Bank of Georgia and TBC (≈75% of banking assets) require non-residents to document source of funds, purpose of account and often "why Georgia"; corporate accounts require proof of business activity, transparent UBO and economic substance/commercial ties to Georgia or its trading partners; rejections are common but second/third attempts with better documents often succeed — [Legal.ge 2026](https://legal.ge/en/news/why-banks-reject-accounts-georgia-2026-en); [PB Services](https://pbservices.ge/blog/common-reasons-georgian-banks-refuse-to-open-accounts-for-foreign-nationals/); [FinancialContent, 26 Sep 2026](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/worldnewswire-2026-9-26-corporate-bank-account-in-georgia-what-foreign-business-owners-need-to-know)
- No residence permit, work visa, local contract or local address is legally required to open a personal account — [PB Services](https://pbservices.ge/blog/common-reasons-georgian-banks-refuse-to-open-accounts-for-foreign-nationals/)
- Work permits (from 1 March 2026 amendments to the Law on the Legal Status of Aliens): most foreigners without permanent residence who perform paid employment, self-employment or entrepreneurial activity in Georgia — including remote employees of Georgian companies, contractors and "business partners who derive financial benefit from activity conducted in Georgia" — need a work permit; exempt: permanent residents and holders of investment residence permits (≥ USD 300,000). Passive shareholding alone does not trigger a permit — [PB Services 2026](https://pbservices.ge/blog/rules-for-foreign-nationals-working-in-georgia-2026/); [Legal.ge](https://legal.ge/en/news/georgia-work-permit-requirements-2026-documents); [Relocation Georgia](https://www.relocationgeorgia.ge/residency/business)

- Sanctions compliance drives bank behaviour: TBC and BoG (both LSE-listed with UK parents) comply with US/EU/UK sanctions and warn they will close accounts for violations; NBG amended its sanctions-compliance regulation — [Forum Free Russia on TBC](https://www.forumfreerussia.org/en/news-en/2023-04-21/large-georgian-bank-tbc-bank-will-close-accounts-of-violators-of-sanctions-against-russia); [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/the-national-bank-of-georgia-and-the-western-sanctions/); [NBG](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/media/news/regulation-on-compliance-with-sanctions-regimes-amended)
- New foreign-funding regime: from 4 March 2026 a legislative package requires organisations and individuals receiving foreign funding to register and report, with potential criminal sanctions for unauthorised cooperation with foreign donors; since April 2025 foreign grants need prior government approval (Anti-Corruption Bureau, up to 3+3 months review), with fines of double the grant for unapproved grants; on 2 March 2026 these mandates moved to the State Audit Office — [HRW, 9 Jul 2026](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/07/09/georgia-new-laws-devastate-independent-civic-groups); [IPI](https://ipi.media/georgia-new-laws-threaten-to-paralyze-independent-media/); [JAMnews grant law](https://jam-news.net/what-new-georgian-law-on-grants-entails/); [Andersen FARA](https://ge.andersen.com/foreign-agents-act-georgia/)
- Mandatory tourist insurance introduced for 2026 visitors (tourism regulatory change) — [BBGeorgiaTravel](https://www.bbgeorgiatravel.com/en/blog/georgia-2026-tourism-updates)

### Inferences
- A foreign founder in 2026 should budget for (a) possible bank-account refusals and 2–6 weeks of KYC, (b) a work permit if actively running the company from Georgia, and (c) legal review of any foreign grant or donor money, which now falls under the grants/FARA regime even for commercial-adjacent projects.
- Local Georgian founders face far fewer frictions; partnering with a Georgian co-founder/director materially eases banking.

## Key Question 5: Political risk — EU path, foreign-agent laws, protests, sanctions, Russia, relocant population

### Takeaway
Georgia's EU accession is frozen ("candidate in name only", Nov 2025), the US and EU have sanctioned officials and Bidzina Ivanishvili, the ruling Georgian Dream is seeking Constitutional Court bans on all parliamentary opposition parties (Sept 2026), and a March 2026 legislative package criminalises unregistered foreign funding — yet macro-stability, the DCFTA and ordinary-citizen visa-free travel to Schengen survive, sanctions on the state or banks have not materialised, and Russia remains the largest tourist market. The 2022 relocant wave has largely thinned out (IT departures in 2025), but a fresh, poorer wave of young Russians began arriving in late July 2026 on mobilisation fears.

- Donor-funded grant programmes for NGOs/civic-tech are effectively closed or high-risk; commercial programmes routed via EU4Business/EBRD/ADB/IFC through banks are the surviving international channel.

## Key Question 7: Credit environment — rates, SME loan availability, bank concentration

### Takeaway
Credit is available but expensive in lari: policy rate 8.25% (Sept 2026), average bank lending rate ~13.9% (Jan 2025) and GEL SME loans typically 12–16%+; credit growth has slowed from 15.5% (Apr 2025) to 12.7% (Oct 2025); the sector is a duopoly (TBC ~37%, BoG ~40% of loans; ~76–78% of assets combined) with heavy foreign-currency SME lending (68%) despite de-dollarisation rules that bar FX loans below GEL 750,000.

### Inferences
- A start-up without collateral or track record will find bank credit costly (mid-teens in GEL) and slow; the realistic early-stage funding stack is own equity + GITA/Enterprise Georgia grants + subsidised Produce in Georgia loans via partner banks, or FX loans only if the ticket exceeds GEL 750k and revenues are FX-linked.
- Duopoly concentration means both banks' compliance postures (sanctions, KYC) effectively set the rules for the whole market; being de-banked by both is existential.
