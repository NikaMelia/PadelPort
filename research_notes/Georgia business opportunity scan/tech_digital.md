## 1. Size and growth of Georgia's IT sector (exports, companies, 2022-23 inflow, Virtual Zone / International Company)

### Takeaway
Georgia's ICT sector roughly quadrupled after 2022, reached ~USD 842m of exports in 2024 (10.9% of services exports) and ~8.2% of nominal GDP, dipped in 2024 when a few large relocated international providers shrank, then re-accelerated to +52% YoY in 2025 (USD 898m in Jan-Sep 2025 alone). Growth is dominated by foreign-owned relocated firms; the local company base is small but grew turnover ~49% in 2024. Some of the 2022-23 Russian/Belarusian/Ukrainian IT inflow has left (one agency estimate: ~3,750 IT specialists left in 2025), but ~100k+ migrants and 37k+ Russian-founded companies remained registered as of 2024-2026, and a new mobilisation scare in Sept 2026 is generating a fresh inflow.

### Cited Findings
**Exports and GDP share**
- ICT exports reached USD 842 million in 2024 = 10.9% of total services exports (Galt & Taggart / Bank of Georgia research, report dated Dec 2025) — [Galt & Taggart IT Sector in Georgia](https://galtandtaggart.com/report/it-sector-in-georgia-2/); [Galt & Taggart PDF, Dec 2025, Eva Bochorishvili](https://ramad.bog.ge/galt/IT-sector-in-Georgia.pdf)
- IT service exports *declined 9.2% YoY in 2024* "due to weaker performance by several large international providers", while smaller international and local companies grew, supporting total sector turnover growth of 48.8% YoY in 2024 — [Investor.ge, 17 Jun 2026: Georgia's technology export sector](https://www.investor.ge/2026/06/17/georgias-technology-export-sector-growth-drivers-and-structural-constraints/)
- ICT exports rose 52.3% YoY in 3Q25; Jan-Sep 2025 ICT exports = USD 898 million — [GBC / Galt & Taggart, 3Q25](https://www.gbc.ge/en/news/Economics-news/ict-exports-surge-523-year-on-year-in-3q25-galt-taggart)
- ICT has been the fastest-growing sector since 2022 and is now the 4th-largest, 8.2% of nominal GDP (Galt & Taggart, Dec 2025) — [Investor.ge, Jun 2026](https://www.investor.ge/2026/06/17/georgias-technology-export-sector-growth-drivers-and-structural-constraints/)

**Virtual Zone Person (VZP) and International Company (IC) statuses**
- VZP grants 0% corporate income tax on profits from export of software/digital services to clients abroad and VAT exemption on service exports; to qualify the company must hold a VZP certificate, perform eligible IT activities, serve clients outside Georgia, and *develop the software within Georgia* (substance requirement) — [Forbes Georgia: VZP vs International IT Company, 2025 tax guide](https://forbes.ge/en/virtual-zone-person-vs-international-it-company-in-georgia-2025-tax-guide-for-it-businesses/); [Andersen Georgia: Virtual Zone Person](https://ge.andersen.com/virtual-zone-person-georgia/); [Gegidze: VZ complete 2026 guide](https://www.gegidze.com/post/georgia-s-virtual-zone-status-for-it-companies-the-complete-2025-guide-to-tax-benefits)
- IC status is a preferential regime for IT and maritime services; the company must have operated (or its group) for at least 2 years; VZP and IC cannot be held simultaneously (IC grant revokes VZP) — [Andersen Georgia: International Company Status](https://ge.andersen.com/international-company-status-georgia/); [Forbes Georgia: which is better](https://forbes.ge/en/tax-incentives-in-georgia-for-it-companies-holding-the-status-of-a-virtual-zone-person-or-an-international-company-which-one-is-better-and-why/)
- Both statuses remain active options in 2025-2026 per multiple service-provider guides — [PB Services VZ blog](https://pbservices.ge/blog/georgia-it-virtual-zone-tax-benefits/); [YB Case](https://ybcase.com/en/jurisdictions/virtualnaa-zona-gruzia)

### Inferences
- The 2024 export dip plus 48.8% turnover growth among smaller firms suggests the sector is bifurcated: a handful of large relocated exporters (whose invoicing decisions swing the national number) and a long tail of small VZP/IC firms and freelancers. A founder's opportunity is in the long tail, and the headline export number is a poor proxy for local demand.
- The Sept 2025 IT residence permit is designed to convert the visa-free/short-stay IT population into longer-term residents; the USD 25k income floor is low relative to IT wages, so uptake should be high if the political climate holds.
- Rent inflation (see also Section 6) is the most-cited reason relocants leave; this is both a failure mode for hiring and a demand signal for housing/relocation services.

### Gaps
- Exact number of registered/active IT companies, VZP certificates issued and IC statuses granted (2023-2026): not retrievable; PMC Research and Galt & Taggart PDFs contain these but were blocked.
- Reconciled ICT export series for 2022 and 2023 on the same definition as the 2024 USD 842m figure.
- Official data on how many relocated IT workers stayed vs. left; only the unverified Voskhod 3,750 figure was found.

## 2. Startup ecosystem: notable startups, exits, VCs, accelerators, ticket sizes, 2024-2026 rounds

### Takeaway
Georgia's startup ecosystem is grant-led (GITA) with one institutional venture programme (500 Global Georgia, USD 20m fund, ~USD 100k average cheque, wound down its accelerator in June 2026), a few small regional angels/VCs (Caucasus Ventures, Georgian Ventures, INMerge), and a handful of internationally recognised startups (Pulsar AI exit 2021; Theneo; Payze via Y Combinator; Keepz; Cargon). Typical tickets are GEL 25k-150k grants and USD 100k-1.5m seed; no verified 2025-2026 round above ~USD 5m was found.

## 3. Fintech and payments (bank dominance, open banking, BNPL, crypto/VASP, remittances, e-money)

### Takeaway
Two banks dominate (TBC alone ~37% of loans and deposits, Sept 2025), and only banks, MFOs and NBG-registered PSPs may offer payment services (EMI minimum capital GEL 1m), yet 60+ fintechs exist and merchants can choose from a dozen-plus gateways offering cards, BNPL, QR, open banking and crypto. Open banking has been an NBG priority since 2020 and the 2025 fintech strategy targets open finance, an upgraded sandbox and an instant-payment system, but there is still no ACH or e-money interoperability layer. Remittances hit USD 3.65bn in 2025 with Russia's share collapsing to 13% (out of the top 10 by Aug 2026). Crypto is regulated through NBG VASP registration (since 2023) with mining untaxed.

### Cited Findings
**Bank dominance and digital banking**
- TBC Bank Georgia holds ~37% of customer loans and 37% of deposits as of 30 Sep 2025; Euromoney named it Georgia's Best Digital Bank 2025 — [Investresearch Substack on TBC](https://investresearch.substack.com/p/tbc-bank-tbcgl-the-georgian-banking); [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/fintech/tbc-bank-best-digital-bank-georgia/)

## 8. Documented failure modes for tech businesses in Georgia

### Takeaway
The best-documented risks are: dependence on a few large relocated exporters (2024 exports -9.2% when they shrank), a small domestic market that forces early foreign re-domiciling or exit, political/regulatory risk since 2024 (Foreign Agents laws, frozen EU path) that is explicitly flagged as threatening investment and the startup ecosystem, rent-driven attrition of relocated talent, and a bank-gated payments/licensing regime. No dedicated post-mortems of failed Georgian startups were found.

### Cited Findings
- Sector concentration: 2024 IT export decline of 9.2% "due to weaker performance by several large international providers" — [Investor.ge, Jun 2026](https://www.investor.ge/2026/06/17/georgias-technology-export-sector-growth-drivers-and-structural-constraints/) (article title itself names "structural constraints")
- Political risk: "Foreign Agents Registration Act" passed 1 Apr 2025, in force 1 Jun 2025, a stricter successor to the 2024 "Law on Transparency of Foreign Influence" that triggered mass protests; it threatens Georgia's EU candidate status (granted Dec 2023) — [FIDH](https://www.fidh.org/en/region/europe-central-asia/georgia/georgia-adoption-of-the-new-foreign-agents-registration-act); [ProtectDefenders](https://protectdefenders.eu/georgias-crackdown-on-civil-society-deepens-as-foreign-agents-law-comes-into-force/); [Atlantic Council](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/blogs/new-atlanticist/its-foreign-agent-bill-caused-an-uproar-now-georgian-dream-has-fast-tracked-another-concerning-law/); [Central Asian Survey 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02634937.2025.2605092)

### Inferences
- Failure pattern for local-market plays: ~3.7m people, two banks controlling payments and distribution, and consumer spend concentrated in Tbilisi make B2C apps sub-scale; the survivors (Keepz, Payze, Balance.ge) are B2B or bank-adjacent.
- Failure pattern for export plays: reliance on relocant talent that leaves when rents rise or politics sour, plus loss of EU-aligned donor money (500 Global exit, foreign-agent law chilling donor programmes).
- Mitigants a founder can control: keep revenue >80% foreign (VZP/IC), design for regional expansion, avoid needing a payments licence, keep team remote-capable so a political shock does not strand the company.
