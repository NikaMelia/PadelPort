## 1. Tourism and hospitality: visitor volumes, spending, source markets, seasonality, hotel performance

### Takeaway
2025 was a record year on headline numbers (5.52m tourist visits, +8.4%; ~7.8m total international visits; USD 4.69bn revenue, +6%), with growth led by the EU/UK (+14%), Gulf and China, while Russia remained the single largest market (~25% of visits in 1Q26). But the picture is bifurcated: Tbilisi branded-hotel occupancy has been mediocre (48-60%) versus Batumi (73%), regional occupancy is ~35%, Q1 2026 was flat (-0.2% visits) as Israel/Iran/Armenia/India flows fell sharply after the Middle East escalation, and Tbilisi hospitality operators report 20-25% fewer restaurant covers. Kutaisi (Wizz Air hub, ~1.8m passengers in 2025) and the Gulf/EU segments are the clearest structural growth vectors.

### Inferences
- Demand is rotating from Russian/Israeli/Iranian/Indian flows toward EU/UK, Gulf, Turkey and China. Businesses tuned to the 2022-24 relocant/Israeli mix (Russian-language service, Israeli-oriented hostels, Iranian shoppers) face headwinds; those serving EU budget flyers via Kutaisi, Gulf families (halal, long stays, summer heat-escape) and Chinese groups are positioned with the trend.
- Tbilisi hotel occupancy in the 48-60% band with ADR ~USD 84-87 suggests the city is *not* under-supplied with generic 3-4* rooms; incremental hotel supply in Tbilisi is a weak opportunity. Batumi branded hotels at 73% and rising are stronger, but face a very large residential/aparthotel pipeline (see Section 4).
- The 35% regional occupancy figure means most regional lodging is heavily seasonal; regional opportunities should be asset-light (glamping, guesthouse upgrades, experiences) or target shoulder-season demand (wine harvest Sept-Oct, ski Dec-Apr) rather than new hotels.
- Kutaisi is the most under-developed hub relative to its passenger throughput (~1.8m via Wizz Air vs a small, transit-oriented hospitality base): airport transfers, short-stay quality lodging, and 1-2 day experience products for EU budget travellers are a visible gap.
- Capital intensity (researcher estimate): guesthouse upgrade/boutique 6-10 rooms in Kakheti or Kutaisi, USD 150-400k; glamping 5-8 units, USD 80-250k; day-tour/transfer operator, USD 30-100k (vehicles); boutique hotel in Tbilisi, USD 1-3m+.

## 2. Food and beverage: restaurants, cafes, chains, specialty coffee, craft beer, delivery

### Takeaway
Georgia's F&B market is expanding on supply (1,100 net new restaurants/cafes/bars opened in 2024 and growth continued in 2025) even as Tbilisi operators report 20-25% fewer covers in 2026; global chains are still thin (McDonald's 4 units, KFC 20+, Wendy's/Dunkin' via Wissol, Starbucks only arriving summer 2026 via Alshaya, Costa targeting 15 stores by 2028), specialty coffee is maturing with internationally awarded roasters (Shavi), craft beer is <15% of a ~GEL 330m beer market dominated by four players, and delivery is a Wolt-led three-way fight at 25-30% commissions with a low-commission entrant (GeraEats, Q4 2025).

### Cited Findings

**Market size, openings, distress**
- By end-2024, 1,100 new restaurants, cafes and bars had opened in Georgia, and the growth trend in catering facilities continued in 2025 — [HoReCa Expo Georgia](https://horecaexpogeorgia.com/en/for-exhibitors/about-the-georgian-market)
- Tbilisi restaurants serving 20-25% fewer visitors y/y; Bushmala fast-food cafe closed; "closures, layoffs and plummeting prices" — [OC Media](https://oc-media.org/how-the-iran-war-is-putting-a-dent-in-georgias-tourism-industry/); [Voxeurop](https://voxeurop.eu/en/georgia-businesses-crisis-russia/)
- Georgia Today analysed restaurant data as revealing "a changing geography of tourism" — [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/beyond-the-numbers-georgias-restaurants-reveal-a-changing-geography-of-tourism/) (content not accessible)
- Restaurant premises for rent in Tbilisi: e.g., 263 m² at USD 3,500/month (~USD 13.3/m²) listed — [Realting](https://realting.com/georgia/property-to-rent/2022516); a listing category exists with more comparables — [Realting restaurants for rent](https://realting.com/georgia/tbilisi/property-to-rent/restaurants)

**Specialty coffee**
- Tbilisi's specialty scene is expanding on consumer interest in coffee education/brew methods; coexistence of traditional and specialty has sped maturation in Tbilisi and Batumi — [Perfect Daily Grind (Jan 2026)](https://perfectdailygrind.com/2026/01/georgia-specialty-coffee-market/)

**Craft beer**
- Georgia's total beer market ~140m litres (~GEL 330m) in 2025, growing ~2%/yr; four players hold ~85%, Efes (Turkey) leads; craft remains a small segment; named craft brewers include Megobrebi, Shavi Lomi, Number 8, Seahorse, Naturale, 9 Mta, Underground, Bayo, Krik, Alkhainadze — [Investor.ge (Aug 2026)](https://www.investor.ge/2026/08/05/from-aludi-to-ipa-how-georgia-is-crafting-a-new-beer-story/)

**Delivery**
- Wolt is the largest platform in Tbilisi and Batumi; Glovo second; Bolt Food re-entered Georgia in 2024 and is growing on rider overlap with Bolt ride-hailing; all three charge restaurants 25-30% plus processing; GeraEats launched in Tbilisi Q4 2025 at a flat 15-18% commission — [GeraEats blog](https://geraeats.com/blog/geraeats-in-georgia-2026) (vendor source, self-interested)
- Glovo and Wolt actively recruiting Bolt's couriers and restaurants (Sep 2025) — [Lider Media](https://en.lider.media/2025/09/09/the-battle-for-bolts-couriers-and-restaurants-glovo-and-wolt-on-the-offensive/)

### Inferences
- The combination of rapidly rising outlet count (1,100 net openings in 2024) and falling covers in 2026 is textbook saturation in tourist-facing Old Town/Sololaki dining. New generic Georgian restaurants or tourist cafes there are a poor bet; the survivors will be concept-led or serve the shifting mix (Gulf families, EU budget travellers, Chinese groups, Turkish business travellers).
- Under-supplied concepts, by inference from the evidence: (a) halal-certified Georgian cuisine (Gulf demand is growing and Arab restaurants have filled the gap, but Georgian-cuisine-with-halal-certification is rarely mentioned); (b) chain-quality QSR outside Tbilisi (McDonald's at 4 units nationally; KFC only just at 20); (c) drive-through and suburban formats; (d) delivery-only/cloud kitchens exploiting 25-30% platform commission arbitrage via own-channel or GeraEats-style lower-commission platforms; (e) healthy/vegan and kids-friendly formats — no market data found either way (see Gaps).
- Starbucks/Costa entering with Gulf franchisees signals the market is now considered big enough for global brands; independents in specialty coffee face brand competition but at a higher price point.
- Craft beer is structurally small (~2% market growth, 85% held by four players); a taproom is a lifestyle business, not a scale play.
- Capital intensity (researcher estimate): specialty cafe USD 60-150k; cloud kitchen USD 30-80k; full-service restaurant 100-150 m² central Tbilisi USD 150-400k (rent ~USD 13-20/m²/month per listings above); QSR franchise USD 300k-1m+ per unit.

### Gaps

- Current (2025-26) Colliers/Cushman retail and F&B rent benchmarks for Tbilisi — not obtained; only listing-level anecdotes.

## 3. Sports, fitness and recreation: padel, tennis, gyms, climbing, yoga, cycling, gaming, kids' activities

### Takeaway
Padel in Georgia is early-stage but real: a national federation (GNPF, FIP-affiliated since 2022), at least 7-9 operators in Tbilisi (PadelHub, Padelbade, Padel Island x2, Kus Tba, Sportscape, Padel Club Georgia, plus Padelio booking platform), Padel Port in Batumi (4 courts) and courts noted in Kutaisi and Rustavi; a directory counts roughly 18 club listings / ~40 courts in Tbilisi (7 indoor, 33 outdoor). Court hire is GEL 40-60/hour, gyms GEL 50-300/month. Globally padel added 7,187 courts in 2024 (+26%) to 50,436, with 92% first-try retention — Georgia's court density is far below Spain/Italy/Sweden/UAE, so the growth thesis is plausible, but the addressable paying population is small and there is no hard Georgian utilisation or player-count data.

## 4. Real estate and property services: short-term rentals, property management, co-living, coworking, relocation

### Takeaway
Short-term rental supply has roughly doubled in Tbilisi (6,777-6,900 active Airbnb listings in 2025 to 12,195 by May 2026) with occupancy ~57% and ADR USD 39-49, while long-term rents fell 12% y/y (USD 10.7/m² Apr 2025) as relocant demand faded; Batumi has ~3,200-9,300 listings (source-dependent), 57% occupancy, USD 43 ADR and ~USD 8k/yr average revenue, against a record 17,052 apartment sales (+17%) in 2025 — i.e. a wave of investor-owned units needing professional management. ## 5. Retail and e-commerce: penetration, marketplaces, cross-border, niches

### Takeaway
E-commerce grew tenfold 2018-2024 to GEL 3.5bn, was forecast at GEL 4.7bn for 2025 and GEL 10.7bn by 2030 (>20% CAGR); Temu, which entered in 2024, took ~50% of transaction volume and ~35% of value in 1H25 and pushed mixed-product marketplaces from 42% to 62% share, squeezing specialised local shops; retail sales grew 16.6% y/y in 4Q25. Structural gaps are delivery speed, regional coverage and courier shortages; local marketplaces (Extra.ge, Veli.store, Vendoo) compete on speed and local assortment.

### Cited Findings
- E-commerce market GEL 3.5bn in 2024 (10x since 2018); projected GEL 4.7bn in 2025 and GEL 10.7bn by 2030 at >20% average annual growth (Galt & Taggart, Dec 2025) — [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/georgias-e-commerce-market-accelerates-toward-gel-10-7-billion-by-2030/); [Galt & Taggart presentation](https://galtandtaggart.com/en/reports/details/69c3a91d46cac30e9b28a4f1)
- Mixed-product marketplaces' share of online purchases rose from 42% (2023) to 62% (1H25), driven by Temu's 2024 entry; in 1H25 Temu was ~50% of transaction volume and ~35% of value (low ticket, direct shipping, aggressive promotions) — [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/georgias-e-commerce-market-accelerates-toward-gel-10-7-billion-by-2030/)
- Veli.store and Extra.ge lead domestic online retail; Temu the most popular foreign platform; also Amazon, eBay, AliExpress, Taobao, Trendyol, Zara, Farfetch; most foreign retailers require freight forwarders (2-3 weeks, customs, 18% VAT above GEL 300) — [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/georgias-e-commerce-market-accelerates-toward-gel-10-7-billion-by-2030/)
- Key challenges: delivery speed, regional coverage, courier shortages (Galt & Taggart) — [Commersant](https://commersant.ge/en/news/business/georgias-e-commerce-markets-key-challenges-remain-delivery-speed-regional-coverage-courier-shortages-galttaggart)
- Business participation in e-commerce lags the EU — [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/georgias-e-commerce-expands-rapidly-but-business-participation-lags-behind-the-eu/)
- Extra.ge: >150,000 products from >2,500 vendors; Veli.store: founded 2021, >100 staff, >30,000 SKUs; Vendoo: TBC Bank-backed, launched 2019 — [Georgia24](https://georgia24.ge/en/article/top-10-e-commerce-websites-in-georgia-overview-of-marketplaces-and-trading-platforms); [Investor.ge](https://www.investor.ge/2023/04/09/veli-the-georgian-e-commerce-startup-delivering-a-better-online-shopping-experience/)

- Pet retail: Tbilisi has both universal pet stores and specialised premium boutiques (Saburtalo, Vake, Old Tbilisi) offering grooming, vet consultations, training and online delivery; named players Animals Market Tbilisi, Bioveti, Tbilisi Pet Shop, Animal World, PetMe, Enzo — [Madloba (2025)](https://madloba.info/en/reviews/top-luchshih-mest-dlya-uhoda-za-zhivotnymi/zoomagaziny-i-vetapteki/top-zoomagazinov-tbilisi/)

### Inferences
- Temu's dominance in low-ticket general merchandise makes it unwise to launch a generic Georgian marketplace or import-and-resell cheap goods. Defensible niches are those Temu cannot serve well: perishable/bulky/time-sensitive categories (groceries, pet food subscriptions, baby essentials, furniture with assembly), items needing after-sales service (electronics repair, appliances), and regulated goods.
- Delivery speed and regional coverage being the top constraint suggests last-mile logistics for regions (Kutaisi, Batumi, Kakheti) and same-day Tbilisi fulfilment are service gaps.
- Capital intensity: niche D2C/e-commerce brand USD 30-100k; regional last-mile courier operation USD 100-300k; electronics repair chain USD 50-150k.

### Gaps
- % of population shopping online, e-commerce share of retail, average basket, and category breakdown — in blocked Galt & Taggart report.
- Market data for second-hand/resale, baby products, furniture, electronics repair — none found.
- 2025 revenue of Extra.ge, Veli, Vendoo — not disclosed in accessible sources.

### Inferences
- The domestic consumer story (double-digit nominal income growth, 16.6% retail growth, ~4-5% inflation) is now stronger than the expat story. Businesses should underwrite on Georgian urban middle-class demand (Tbilisi/Batumi/Kutaisi households at GEL 2,200+/month, top quintile substantially higher) rather than on relocants.
- The relocant purchasing-power shock is largely spent: net Russian inflow ~0 in 2025, remittances -88%. Russian-language premium services (coworking, cafes, kids' clubs) that boomed 2022-23 are the most exposed segment for 2026-27; several closures are implied by the "long-term rentals disappearing/rents -12%" data but no closure list was found.
- Card acceptance is near-universal for POS but cash remains dominant by value — consumer businesses should still accept cash, and there is room for fintech/BNPL in ticket sizes above everyday spend.
- Rising food inflation (8%) compresses restaurant margins in 2026, consistent with the operator distress reported in Section 2.

### Gaps
- Income distribution/top-decile spending, Tbilisi-specific income, and consumer-confidence indices — not obtained (Geostat pages blocked).
- Size of the remaining expat/relocant population by nationality (stock, not flow) — census breakdown was in a blocked Civil.ge article.
- Spending patterns of Gulf and Chinese long-stay visitors — no quantitative data.

## 9. Cross-cutting opportunity shortlist (researcher synthesis for the report writer)

Ranked by strength of evidence, not by expected return. Capital figures are researcher estimates unless cited above.

1. **Indoor/covered padel club in Tbilisi (and hotel-partnered courts in Batumi)** — Evidence: ~40 courts in Tbilisi but only ~7 indoor ([MyPadelWay](https://mypadelway.com/ge/city/tbilisi)); active federation; GEL 40-60/hr pricing ([PadelHub](https://www.padelhub.ge/)); 92% global retention and +26% global court growth ([SGB/Playtomic](https://sgbonline.com/exec-study-predicts-continued-explosive-growth-for-padel-including-in-the-u-s/)). Risk: small paying base, low price ceiling. Capex USD 400k-1m for 4-6 indoor courts.
2. **Property/STR management and rental-pool operator in Batumi** — 17,052 units sold in 2025 into a market with ~USD 8k/yr per STR ([Colliers via Eva Estate](https://www.eva-estate.com/news/issue.asp?id=39); [Airbtics](https://airbtics.com/annual-airbnb-revenue-in-batumi-georgia/)). Asset-light, USD 20-60k.
3. **Kutaisi-hub services for EU budget travellers** — ~1.8m Wizz Air passengers, 99.7% loads, transit-city status, ~35% regional occupancy ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/travel/2026/01/31/natural-wine-long-hikes-and-history-could-kutaisi-be-georgias-next-great-travel-destinatio)). Transfers, day tours, quality short-stay lodging. USD 50-400k.
4. **Gulf-family-oriented hospitality and halal Georgian dining** — GCC visits 145,513 (+3.4%), Saudi +9.1% in 2025 and +36.6% in 1Q26; multi-month stays ([1TV](https://1tv.ge/lang/en/news/national-tourism-administration-georgia-welcomes-record-5-5-million-tourists-in-2025/); [Civil Georgia](https://civil.ge/archives/732723)). USD 100-500k.
5. **Regional last-mile logistics / same-day fulfilment** — top e-commerce constraint per Galt & Taggart ([Commersant](https://commersant.ge/en/news/business/georgias-e-commerce-markets-key-challenges-remain-delivery-speed-regional-coverage-courier-shortages-galttaggart)); market to GEL 10.7bn by 2030. USD 100-300k.
6. **Low-commission or own-channel food delivery / cloud kitchens** — incumbents at 25-30% commission ([GeraEats](https://geraeats.com/blog/geraeats-in-georgia-2026)). USD 30-80k per kitchen.
7. **Medical/dental tourism aggregation targeting Gulf, EU (via Kutaisi) and Central Asia** — crowded clinic supply, thin demand-generation; packages USD 2,900-9,660 ([Medical Tourism Co](https://www.medicaltourismco.com/clinics/dentist/georgia/)). USD 20-50k.
8. **Destination-wedding venue + legal facilitation** — agencies report exponential demand into 2026 ([Shu Wedding](https://shuwedd.com/blog/what-documents-do-foreigners-need-to-get-married-in-georgia/)); vendor-sourced, unquantified. USD 300k-1m for venue; USD 10-30k for agency.

**Weak or over-supplied (avoid):** generic Old Tbilisi restaurants/cafes (1,100 openings in 2024, covers -20-25% in 2026); new Tbilisi STR units (listings doubled to 12,195, ADR USD 49); generic 3-4* Tbilisi hotels (48-60% occupancy); general-merchandise e-commerce vs Temu; generic coworking (13-23 spaces already); Russian-relocant-dependent services (net inflow ~0, remittances -88%).
