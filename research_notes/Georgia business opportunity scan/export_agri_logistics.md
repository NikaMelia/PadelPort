## 1. Trade profile: top exports and markets 2023-2025, growth, and the car re-export question

### Takeaway
Georgia's exports hit a record ~USD 7.29 bn in 2025 (+11.2%), but more than half is re-exports, dominated by cars routed to Central Asia (and, per mirror-data analyses, onward to Russia). Domestic exports were only ~USD 3.37 bn, led by precious-metal ores/copper concentrate, ferroalloys, wine, hazelnuts, and mineral water; Russia was the single largest destination for domestic goods in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- Preliminary Geostat data: total exports USD 7.29 bn in 2025, +11.2% y/y; domestic exports USD 3.37 bn = 46.2% of total (i.e., re-exports ~53.8%); leading destinations for domestic exports were Russia USD 700.7 m, China USD 301.9 m, Türkiye USD 298.8 m — [Caspian Post citing Geostat, 2025 annual](https://caspianpost.com/georgia/georgia-s-exports-hit-record-usd-7-2-billion-in-2025)
- Jan-Apr 2025: precious-metal ores and concentrates were the No. 1 domestic export at USD 96.3 m (10.7% of domestic exports); top domestic-export partners Russia USD 173.4 m, Türkiye USD 87.9 m, China USD 77.8 m — [BM.ge citing Geostat](https://bm.ge/en/news/georgias-domestic-exports-slightly-up-geostat)
- Geostat publishes a dedicated "Domestic Exports" series (primary source for verification) — [Geostat](https://www.geostat.ge/en/modules/categories/639/domestic-exports)
- Car re-exports: light vehicles worth USD 1.2 bn were re-exported in H1 2025, 38% of Georgia's total exports; car re-exports up 493% since 2021 — [IDFI study, 2025](https://idfi.ge/en/to-central-asia-or-russia-record-growth-in-car-re-exports); also reported by [Jamestown Foundation](https://jamestown.org/record-car-re-exports-from-georgia-raise-sanctions-evasion-concerns/)
- Mirror-data gap: Georgian statistics show cars worth USD 964 m re-exported to Kyrgyzstan in Jan-Oct 2024, while Kyrgyz statistics record only ~USD 50 m arriving from Georgia; the discrepancy is read as evidence the cars end up in Russia — [Jamestown](https://jamestown.org/record-car-re-exports-from-georgia-raise-sanctions-evasion-concerns/); corroborating analysis by [Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/georgia-and-kyrgyzstan-suspected-of-being-auto-suppliers-for-russia-trade-data-indicates)
- Policy response: Georgia banned re-export of certain EU-made cars to Russia/Belarus in 2023; in May 2025 the ban was clarified to also apply to Russian and Belarusian citizens as exporters — [Mezha](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/georgia-bans-car-exports-by-russian-and-belarusian-citizens-to-prevent-sanctions-evasion/); background on the 2023 ban in [JAMnews](https://jam-news.net/georgia-emerges-as-key-route-for-car-supplies-to-russia-amid-sanctions/)
- Hazelnuts: 2025 exports reported at 20,000 t worth USD 171.7 m (see Section 3 for conflicting figures) — [Georgia Today citing MEPA](https://georgiatoday.ge/hazelnut-export-value-rises-despite-drop-in-volume-ministry/)
- Wine: 2025 exports 89.7 m litres worth USD 267.9 m to 71 countries (see Section 4) — [Wine Intelligence](https://wine-intelligence.com/blogs/wine-analytics-pricing-report-data/georgia-reports-strong-wine-and-spirits-export-performance-in-2025)

### Inferences
- Headline export growth overstates tradable-sector health: strip out cars and domestic exports are ~USD 3.4 bn, of which metals/ores (volatile, few players) plus wine, hazelnuts and water are the core. A founder should benchmark against the domestic-export series, not total exports.
- The car re-export trade is large, low-margin, and carries acute sanctions-enforcement and reputational risk (EU 14th/15th package-style circumvention scrutiny; potential secondary sanctions on banks/forwarders). It is not a sound greenfield business for a Western-facing founder, though ancillary services (bonded storage, compliance/KYC screening, inspection) may be defensible.
- Russia's re-emergence as the No. 1 market for domestically produced goods (USD 700 m in 2025) is itself a concentration risk given wine and water exposure (Section 4).

### Gaps
- Could not obtain the full 2025 top-10 domestic export product table (ferroalloys, copper ores, mineral water, fertilizers, fruit values) — Geostat/BM.ge pages blocked.
- No 2023-2024 full-year comparison series retrieved.
- No full-year 2025 car re-export value by destination (Kyrgyzstan/Kazakhstan/Azerbaijan/Armenia) beyond the H1 USD 1.2 bn figure.

## 2. Free trade access: DCFTA, China, Türkiye, EFTA, CIS, UK, Hong Kong, GSP; which products realistically exploit them

### Takeaway
Georgia's tariff-free access to the EU (DCFTA), China/Hong Kong, Türkiye, EFTA, CIS and the UK, plus GSP into the US/Canada/Japan, is real and unusual, but in practice only a short list of products has converted it into volume: hazelnuts and blueberries (EU), wine (EU, China, US), herbs/bay leaf, and cut-make-trim apparel to Türkiye/EU.

### Cited Findings
- Georgia has the DCFTA with the EU, FTAs with Türkiye, Ukraine, China (incl. Hong Kong), EFTA and CIS countries, and GSP with the USA, Canada and Japan — [Georgian Textile Group](https://www.georgiantextilegroup.com/georgia); the same FTA/GSP list is cited in FIZ marketing — [ExpatHub](https://expathub.ge/0-tax-with-conditions-free-industrial-zones-of-georgia/)
- Hazelnuts and blueberries together made up almost 95% of all Georgian fruit-and-vegetable exports to the EU (snippet context: mid-2026 data; blueberry shipments to the EU rose from 69 t to 91 t in June 2026 y/y) — [FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/9869591/hazelnuts-continue-to-drive-georgian-exports-to-the-eu/)
- From 1 Aug 2025 to 31 Mar 2026 Georgia exported 10,700 t of hazelnuts to EU countries; the average export price Aug-Sep 2025 was USD 8.46/kg, +39% y/y — [MEPA](https://mepa.gov.ge/En/News/Details/24142)
- Georgian honey producers gained EU market access under the DCFTA (listing achieved; date not captured in snippet) — [DCFTA.gov.ge](http://www.dcfta.gov.ge/en/news/98/Georgian-Honey-Producers-can-export-to-the-EU-market); MEPA issued national safe-honey production guidelines — [MEPA](https://mepa.gov.ge/En/News/Details/11748)
- Since provisional application of the DCFTA Georgia began exporting new products to the EU including dried fruits and berries — [3DCFTAs](https://3dcftas.eu/op-eds/eu-georgia-dcfta-results-after-5-years-and-perspectives)
- Fresh herbs: ~1,900 t worth USD 4.5 m exported in H1 2026 — [EastFruit tag page, Georgia](https://east-fruit.com/en/tag/georgia/)
- Bay leaf: demand for top-quality Georgian bay leaf "has skyrocketed"; an EBRD/EU-supported producer had to expand to meet export requirements — [EastFruit](https://east-fruit.com/en/horticultural-business/the-ebrd-and-the-eu-help-boost-competitiveness-of-bay-leaf-producer-in-georgia/)
- Wine to China: USD 9.8 m in 2025 (+6%) among Georgia's listed markets — [Wine Intelligence](https://wine-intelligence.com/blogs/wine-analytics-pricing-report-data/georgia-reports-strong-wine-and-spirits-export-performance-in-2025)
- Wine to the US grew at a 15.5% CAGR 2021-2024 with a 2025 "Georgia, Naturally" trade/consumer campaign — [Wine Industry Advisor](https://wineindustryadvisor.com/2025/12/09/growing-2025-exports-to-the-u-s-underpinning-georgias-strong-sales-momentum/)
- Apparel: Georgia exported USD 103.78 m of knitted/crocheted apparel to Türkiye in 2024 — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/georgia/exports/turkey/articles-apparel-accessories-knit-crocheted)
- The EU frames the DCFTA as legal approximation that "expands the EU market to Georgian goods and services" — [EU DG Trade](https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/eu-trade-relationships-country-and-region/countries-and-regions/georgia_en)

### Inferences
- The binding constraint on DCFTA use is not tariffs but SPS/food-safety compliance (EU-listed establishments, residue testing, traceability) and volume consistency; honey is a case where market access exists but exports remain tiny, indicating room for an aggregator/exporter that can achieve EU certification for smallholders.
- Realistic new-entrant niches under DCFTA: blueberries (proven, fast-growing), dried fruit/nuts and herbs/spices (low capital, but need HACCP/organic certification), honey (access exists, supply fragmented), bay leaf (established demand). Kiwi, tea, and mineral water were not evidenced in retrieved data.
- China FTA has mostly benefited wine; the 2025 China wine figure (USD 9.8 m) is small relative to Russia, so China is a diversification play rather than a volume play.

### Gaps
- No 2025 figures found for kiwi, tea, mineral water, dried fruit, furniture or textiles to the EU specifically (searches did not return them before quota exhaustion).
- Did not retrieve the EU's 2025 DCFTA implementation report or any statement on whether EU-Georgia trade preferences are affected by the 2024-2026 political rift.
- UK and EFTA agreement utilisation figures not found.

## 3. Agriculture and agri-processing: high-value crops, costs, cold chain, state programs, land rules

### Takeaway
Blueberries and hazelnuts are the two proven high-value export crops with 2025 data showing volume and price growth; a new RDA "State Program of Agro-Financing" (from 1 July 2026) co-finances up to 50% of greenhouse, orchard, livestock, apiculture and aquaculture projects (max GEL 2 m), but foreigners cannot own agricultural land and must lease (up to 49 years) or partner with a Georgian-majority company.

### Cited Findings
**Blueberries**
- 2025 season: production exceeded 7,500 t, ~95% exported (~7,000 t); main destinations Russia, Germany, Poland, Dubai; trial shipments to India; 2026 targets India, Israel, Saudi Arabia and more EU markets — [FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/9814617/georgia-s-blueberry-exports-expand-as-sector-targets-eu-growth-and-new-markets/)
- Georgian blueberry exports grew 660% in four years (to 2024) — [International Blueberry Organization, Jan 2025](https://www.internationalblueberry.org/2025/01/02/georgian-blueberry-exports-grow-660-in-four-years/)
- Galt & Taggart has a dedicated "Blueberry Business in Georgia" sector report (content not retrievable) — [Galt & Taggart](https://galtandtaggart.com/en/reports/details/69c3a1d2b8eea120bf64877c)
- Exporters are working to "establish a reputation" as one of the newest producers — [IBO, Mar 2025](https://www.internationalblueberry.org/2025/03/07/georgias-blueberry-exporters-aim-to-build-on-promising-start-to-establish-a-reputation-for-excellence-as-one-of-the-globes-newest-and-best-producers/)

**Hazelnuts**
- Reported 2025 exports: 20,000 t worth USD 171.7 m — [Georgia Today citing MEPA](https://georgiatoday.ge/hazelnut-export-value-rises-despite-drop-in-volume-ministry/); **conflicting/partial figures:** USD 100.5 m in the first 9 months (year not stated in snippet) — [Commersant](https://commersant.ge/en/news/economics/georgia-exported-hazelnuts-worth-1005-million-in-9-months) and [BM.ge](https://bm.ge/en/news/georgia-earned-usd-1005-mln-from-hazelnut-exports-in-the-last-9-months); "exports up 20%" — [GBC](https://gbc.ge/en/news/Economics-news/hazelnut-exports-from-georgia-up-by-20)
- Aug-Sep 2025 average export price USD 8.46/kg (+39% y/y); Italy the largest buyer (1,000 t in Aug-Sep 2025); 10,700 t to EU from 1 Aug 2025 to 31 Mar 2026 — [MEPA](https://mepa.gov.ge/En/News/Details/24142)
- Main markets: EU (Italy, Germany, Spain, France, Greece), China, USA — [BTU AI](https://btuai.ge/en/georgias-hazelnut-export-growth-and-global-market-trends/)
- Industry target (set before 2025) of 100,000 t production by 2025 — [EastFruit](https://east-fruit.com/en/news/georgian-nut-growers-plan-to-increase-hazelnut-production-to-100-000-tonnes-by-2025-video/) (the 20,000 t export figure implies this target was far from met)
- Sector critique of Georgia's nut industry's ability to "crack the global market" — [Investor.ge, Apr 2025](https://www.investor.ge/2025/04/06/can-georgias-nut-industry-crack-the-global-market/)

**Other**
- Fresh herbs ~1,900 t / USD 4.5 m in H1 2026 — [EastFruit](https://east-fruit.com/en/tag/georgia/)
- Fruit exporters diversifying (blueberries, tomato beer, etc.) — [Investor.ge, Feb 2025](https://www.investor.ge/2025/02/10/from-blueberries-to-tomato-beer-georgias-fruit-farmers-find-firmer-footing-abroad/)

**State programs (RDA)**
- "State Program of Agro-Financing" launched 1 July 2026, run by the Rural Development Agency: state co-financing up to 50% of project cost, max GEL 2 m per project; 55% for agricultural cooperatives, highland settlements and villages near the occupation line; eligible: perennial crops, greenhouses, animal and poultry farms, beekeeping, aquaculture and mariculture; applications online via RDA — [GeorgiaOnline](https://georgiaonline.ge/georgia-launches-state-agro-co-financing-program/); RDA awareness drive — [GeorgiaOnline](https://georgiaonline.ge/rda-launches-awareness-drive-on-state-agro-financing-program/)
- RDA "Preferential Agrocredit Project" page exists (terms not retrievable) — [RDA](https://rda.gov.ge/en/programs/541134-preferential-agrocredit-project/5bfb2c56-3c6f-4a61-a418-06bd2d90ff87/)

**Land rules for foreigners**
- Foreign individuals and foreign-owned legal entities may not acquire agricultural land (constitutional restriction); the main route is long-term lease up to 49 years — [Legal.ge](https://legal.ge/en/service/civil-law-en/agricultural-land-transactions-en); [Sea-Inside guide](https://sea-inside.net/en/can-foreigners-buy-land-in-georgia-legal-guide/)
- A Georgian-registered company majority-owned by Georgian citizens is treated as domestic and may buy agricultural land even with a foreign minority shareholder; land-status conversion (agricultural to non-agricultural) is possible but needs municipal/state approval; nominee ownership is illegal and void — [BuyHome.ge](https://buyhome.ge/restrictions-for-foreigners-in-georgia-land-agricultural-use-and-workarounds); [Orson Co](https://www.orsonco.com/can-foreigners-own-land-in-georgia-legal-rules-explained/)

### Inferences
- Blueberries: capital intensity is high (drip irrigation, netting, cold chain, GlobalG.A.P.) and time-to-revenue ~3 years to first commercial crop; but 95% export ratio and diversifying markets make it the strongest evidence-backed agri play. Russia's role as a top destination is a risk to underwrite.
- Hazelnuts: a price-recovery year in 2025 (USD 8.46/kg) after years of disease/quality problems; the opportunity is in processing/kernel grading and traceability for EU buyers rather than new orchards (7-year time-to-yield).
- Honey/herbs/bay leaf/dried fruit: low capital (USD tens of thousands to low hundreds), 6-18 months to revenue, but margin depends on obtaining EU certification and aggregating fragmented smallholder supply.
- The July 2026 RDA program appears to consolidate earlier instruments; a founder structuring a Georgian-majority JV could access up to GEL 2 m (~USD 0.7 m) co-financing for a greenhouse or aquaculture project.

- Medical cannabis legal status 2025-2026: not researched (query blocked by quota).
- Typical land lease prices, greenhouse capex per hectare, and cold-storage capacity/gaps for Georgia: no Georgia-specific figures surfaced (one search returned US-state and Indian data only).
- Organic certification bodies/uptake: not found.
- Whether the 2026 Agro-Financing program replaced "Plant the Future" or preferential agrocredit, and whether agricultural insurance subsidies were cut: not confirmed (searches returned US-state results).

## 4. Wine and spirits: exports 2024-2025 by market, Russia dependence, natural/qvevri trend, room for new entrants

### Takeaway
Georgia exported 89.7 m litres of wine worth ~USD 268 m to 71 countries in 2025 with average price rising to USD 2.98/L, but roughly 60-66% still goes to Russia, where volumes fell sharply in H1 2025 (-1.5x); EU sales are small (~USD 39 m across ~30 countries) but growing in value, and the US is the fastest-growing Western market.

### Cited Findings
- 2025: 89.7 m litres, USD 267.9 m, 71 countries; average price USD 2.98/L vs USD 2.91/L in 2024; "modest declines in volume" — [Wine Intelligence](https://wine-intelligence.com/blogs/wine-analytics-pricing-report-data/georgia-reports-strong-wine-and-spirits-export-performance-in-2025)
- 2024: USD 276.1 m to 72 countries; Jan-Nov 2025 USD 245 m — [Parliament of Georgia, NWA report](https://www.parliament.ge/en/media/news/soflis-meurneobis-komitetma-ghvinis-erovnuli-saagentos-sakmianobis-angarishi-moismina) (note: 2025 full-year USD 267.9 m from Wine Intelligence vs USD 276.1 m in 2024 implies a ~3% value decline; the two sources use different bases so treat as approximate)
- **Conflicting series:** Geostat-based figures cited by Commersant: 67,535 t worth USD 195.8 m exported in 2024, and 56,219 t worth USD 164.9 m in Jan-Aug 2025 — [Commersant](https://commersant.ge/en/news/business/georgian-wine-exports-down-by-about-16-the-main-export-market-is-russia). These are materially lower than NWA/Wine-Intelligence values; likely differing HS coverage (bottled wine only vs. incl. bulk/other), unverified.
- Europe 2025: 11.5 m litres worth USD 38.7 m to ~30 European countries; Poland USD 17 m (+2%), Germany USD 6 m (+1%), Latvia USD 4.7 m (+16%), Lithuania USD 2.6 m (+5%), France USD 0.83 m (+63%) — [National Wine Agency](https://wine.gov.ge/En/News/38364); other 2025 markets: China USD 9.8 m (+6%), Canada USD 1.3 m (+24%), Israel USD 1.14 m (+23%), Japan USD 1.13 m (+34%) — [Wine Intelligence](https://wine-intelligence.com/blogs/wine-analytics-pricing-report-data/georgia-reports-strong-wine-and-spirits-export-performance-in-2025)
- Russia share 62.6% of wine exports in Jan-Aug 2025 — [Commersant](https://commersant.ge/en/news/business/georgian-wine-exports-down-by-about-16-the-main-export-market-is-russia); "66% Russian dependency" and "15.8% export collapse" framing — [En Primeur Club](https://www.enprimeurclub.com/guide/georgian-wine-western-pivot-russia-dependency-export-collapse)
- Q1 2025 exports to Russia fell 2.4x to USD 27.6 m, volume down 2.5x to 9.7 kt — [Tridge](https://insights.tridge.com/speaker-product-news-reports/KJLyFXpzJzgKuB1Gqsm5QUKHGgR43AVdqLEDeBUXweDH2CKLqtLed3wK); H1 2025 exports to Russia 26,500 t, a 1.54x decrease y/y (Geostat) — [TASS](https://tass.com/economy/1992195)
- US: 15.5% CAGR 2021-2024; 2025 "Georgia, Naturally" campaign — [Wine Industry Advisor](https://wineindustryadvisor.com/2025/12/09/growing-2025-exports-to-the-u-s-underpinning-georgias-strong-sales-momentum/)
- NWA presented Georgian wine in 21 countries at ~60 exhibitions/festivals in 2025 — [Rustavi2](https://rustavi2.ge/en/news/336836)
- Natural-wine exports reportedly declined in 2025 with producers making "strategic adjustments" — [BTU AI](https://btuai.ge/en/georgian-natural-wine-export-decline-in-2025-challenges-and-strategic-adjustments/) (secondary source, unverified)
- Forbes (Jun 2026) frames Georgian wine as "caught between Russia and the West" — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michellewilliams/2026/06/11/georgian-wine-is-caught-between-russia-and-the-west/)
- Historical context: after the 2013 lifting of Russia's embargo, 65 companies were licensed to resume Russian exports — [GBC](https://www.gbc.ge/en/news/Economics-news/georgia-ranks-3rd-among-the-russias-main-wine-suppliers)

### Inferences
- The market is bifurcated: bulk/semi-sweet volume to Russia (falling, politically exposed, paid in a sanctioned banking environment) vs. premium qvevri/natural wine to EU/US/Japan (growing 20-60%/yr in some markets but from tiny bases). New small producers and export brokers have room in the second segment; the first is dominated by large incumbents.
- Rising average price (USD 2.98/L) with flat/declining volume is consistent with premiumisation, which favours small-batch entrants.
- Capital: a small qvevri winery is low-to-mid capital (vineyard lease + cellar; multi-year to revenue), while an export brokerage/importer-of-record model into EU/US is low capital and 6-12 months to revenue but margin-thin and dependent on distributor access. Wine tourism (Kakheti) piggybacks on tourism growth but was not quantified here.

## 5. Manufacturing: apparel/textiles, FIZs, light assembly, labour costs

### Takeaway
Georgia's manufacturing export story is essentially Adjara's CMT apparel cluster (Adjara Textile: ~7,000 employees, >10 m units/yr, revenue GEL 333.6 m) selling to Turkish and EU brands, plus four Free Industrial Zones offering 0% VAT/CIT/dividend tax; no hard 2025 wage comparison with Türkiye/Armenia was retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- Adjara became a textile destination by offering cheaper labour than Türkiye, visible by 2015 when Türkiye lost its cost advantage — [ISET Policy Institute](https://iset-pi.ge/en/blog/252-the-textile-industry-stopping-by-in-georgia)
- Adjara Textile: revenue rose to GEL 333.6 m (year not captured in snippet); ~7,000 employed across factories in Poti, Batumi and Kobuleti; >10 m units/yr for global brands — [Commersant](https://commersant.ge/en/news/business/adjara-textiles-income-soared-to-gel-3336-million); Ajara Textile setting up a new factory — [Fibre2Fashion](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/news/apparel-news/georgia-s-ajara-textile-to-set-up-new-apparel-factory-268643-newsdetails.htm)
- Brands produced in Georgia: Marks & Spencer, Moncler, Nike, Adidas, Zara, Puma, H&M, Tommy Hilfiger, Koton; largest firms do CMT — [Georgian Textile Group](https://www.georgiantextilegroup.com/georgia); [Kohan Textile Journal](https://kohantextilejournal.com/textile-and-apparel-industry-in-georgia-a-history-of-growth-and-potential/)
- Knitted apparel exports to Türkiye USD 103.78 m in 2024 — [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/georgia/exports/turkey/articles-apparel-accessories-knit-crocheted)
- Georgia has no statutory minimum wage regulation affecting private-sector labour costs (per industry source) — [Kohan Textile Journal](https://kohantextilejournal.com/textile-and-apparel-industry-in-georgia-a-history-of-growth-and-potential/)
- Four FIZs operational as of 2025; main ones Poti (port), Kutaisi (manufacturing), Tbilisi (trade/logistics) — [Company Formation Georgia](https://companyformationgeorgia.ge/free-industrial-zones-in-georgia/); FIZ residents exempt from VAT (18%), CIT (15%) and dividend tax (5%) — [ExpatHub](https://expathub.ge/0-tax-with-conditions-free-industrial-zones-of-georgia/); legal basis — [Law on Free Industrial Zones](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/21994); zone comparison — [PB Services](https://pbservices.ge/blog/free-zones-in-georgia-difference/), [Kutaisi Free Zone](https://kutaisifreezone.ge/free-zones-in-georgia/)
- FABTEX Georgia trade fair exists (2nd edition) — [FABTEX](https://www.fabtexgeorgia.com/)

### Inferences
- CMT apparel is a proven, labour-arbitrage business tied to Turkish supply chains; new entrants face concentration risk (one dominant group, Turkish-owned supply) and would need to bring their own brand relationships. Time-to-revenue is short (6-12 months) but capex for a 200-300-machine line is moderate (low USD millions).
- FIZs suit re-export/light-assembly models (Central Asia or EU-bound) where inputs are imported and finished goods leave Georgia; sales into Georgia attract a 4% levy (commonly cited in FIZ guides; not verified from a primary source here).
- Furniture, construction materials, packaging and pharmaceuticals: no 2025 evidence retrieved; treat as unresearched.

### Gaps
- Average manufacturing wages 2025 (Geostat) and comparison with Türkiye and Armenia: not found.
- Number of companies and investment volumes in Poti, Kutaisi, Hualing and Tbilisi FIZs: not found.
- 2025 textile export totals: not found.

## 6. Logistics and the Middle Corridor: volumes, Anaklia, Poti/Batumi, rail, missing services

### Takeaway
Middle Corridor transit through Georgia roughly doubled 2018-2025 to ~4.6-5 m tonnes, with container transit up 33% in 2025 and APM Terminals Poti at a record >600,000 TEU; Poti is at capacity, while Anaklia's Chinese partner (CCCC) exited by mid-2026, the state moved to a multi-partner "landlord" model, 2026 Anaklia funding was cut from GEL 150 m to GEL 50 m, and operations are not expected before 2029.

### Cited Findings
- Middle Corridor transit through Georgia doubled 2018-2025 to 4.6 m t — [PMCG](https://pmcg-i.com/research/middle-corridor-transit-cargo-flows-georgia/); ~5 m t in 2025, expected +10%/yr to 2030 — [Carnegie Endowment, Apr 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/russia-eurasia/politika/2026/04/middle-corridor-transport-prospect) (Carnegie's headline argues the corridor "could prove a dead end")

### Inferences
- Port capacity, not demand, is the binding constraint through at least 2029. That favours asset-light services that relieve congestion: off-dock container yards and bonded warehousing near Poti/Kutaisi, empty-container depots, rail-truck transload, customs brokerage with digital pre-clearance, and Caspian ferry slot brokerage. These are low-to-mid capital (USD 0.5-5 m) with 6-18 months to revenue.
- Anaklia's repeated partner failures (2020 ADC cancellation; 2026 CCCC exit) are the sector's headline failure case; do not build a business plan that depends on Anaklia before 2030.
- Geopolitical risk: Carnegie's "dead end" thesis, Russia-Ukraine settlement scenarios reviving the Northern Corridor, and EU-Georgia political tensions all bear on corridor volumes.

## 7. Energy: small hydro/solar, net metering, GENEX, EVs and charging

### Takeaway
Georgia launched a Nord Pool-run day-ahead/intraday exchange (GENEX) in July 2024 and mandatory hourly balancing from 1 July 2025, creating a transparent price for small generators; EV imports jumped 151% to 5,402 units in 2024 (duty-free, increasingly Chinese) and Tbilisi installed 130 of 198 planned fast chargers in 2025 after an auction where bids rose 11.5x.

### Cited Findings
- GENEX (JSC, est. 2019 by GSE and ESCO) operates day-ahead, intraday and bilateral markets; first day-ahead auction 1 July 2024 — [Nord Pool](https://www.nordpoolgroup.com/en/message-center-container/newsroom/exchange-message-list/2024/q3/genex-and-nord-pool-launch-georgian-power-market/); [GSE](https://www.gse.com.ge/communication/news/2024/Electricity-Exchange-was-launched-in-Georgia); [GENEX](https://genex.ge/en)
- Participation voluntary, access limited to defined entities; regulated large HPPs and traders excluded from the exchange; hourly balancing and ancillary services market mandatory from 1 July 2025 (GSE) — [Energy Community](https://www.energy-community.org/news/Energy-Community-News/2024/07/01b.html); [GSE](https://www.gse.com.ge/communication/news/2024/Electricity-Exchange-was-launched-in-Georgia)
- Energy Community 2025 implementation report for Georgia (full text blocked) — [Energy Community IR 2025](https://www.energy-community.org/dam/jcr:77cd9d2d-6d71-418e-8235-4d7a5c6ddf8c/Georgia_IR25CP.pdf)
- Opportunities in hydro, wind and solar generation cited by US Commerce; exchange expected to attract investment and make pricing transparent — [Trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/georgia-electrical-power-systems-elp)
- Georgia updated net-metering rules for microgeneration (EBRD GEFF summary; caps and dates not captured in snippet) — [EBRD GEFF Georgia](https://ebrdgeff.com/georgia/en/english-georgia-updates-net-metering-rules-for-microgeneration/)
- IEA Georgia energy profile and sustainable-transport roadmap — [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/georgia-energy-profile/overview); [IEA transport](https://www.iea.org/reports/sustainable-transport-for-georgia-a-roadmap/sustainable-transport-roadmap)
- EV imports: 5,402 units in 2024, +151% y/y, decade record; average import price ~USD 19,500 (2024) vs USD 25,900 (2023); EVs exempt from import customs duty; Chinese brands gaining share — [Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/as-georgias-electric-vehicle-market-grows-china-powers-ahead)
- Tbilisi installed 130 of 198 planned fast chargers in 2025 after auction bids jumped 11.5x — [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/tbilisi-installs-130-fast-ev-chargers-after-auction-bids-jump-11-5x/); Electrify Georgia rolling out 150 kW+ chargers on intercity routes — [Forbes Georgia](https://forbes.ge/en/powering-a-greener-georgia-how-electrify-georgia-is-leading-the-ev-revolution-in-tbilisi/)

### Inferences
- Small solar (rooftop/commercial) for self-consumption plus net metering is the lowest-capital energy entry (USD 50-500k, revenue immediate via avoided cost), but the exact capacity cap and credit terms must be confirmed from GNERC/GEFF.
- The 11.5x jump in charger-auction bids signals that prime urban charging locations are already competitive; opportunity shifts to intercity/tourist corridors and destination charging (hotels, wineries).
- Small hydro faces long permitting and local opposition; not evidenced here as an attractive small-investor niche.

## 8. Construction and real estate: prices, volumes, Batumi oversupply

### Takeaway
Prices kept rising in 2025 (Tbilisi asking prices +8% y/y in June 2025; Batumi primary-market average USD 1,865/m2, +9.4%), but Batumi faces a 58,000-apartment pipeline for 2025-2029 with rising unsold stock and falling rental yields, so analysts expect slower price growth in 2026 and weakness in non-premium coastal high-rises.

### Cited Findings
- June 2025: Tbilisi residential asking prices +8% y/y, Batumi +12% — [Investor.ge, Oct 2025](https://www.investor.ge/2025/10/23/from-coast-to-capital-rising-costs-reshape-georgias-property-market/)

## 9. Incentives: Produce in Georgia, Enterprise Georgia, agricultural programs; what changed in 2025-2026

### Takeaway
Produce in Georgia was expanded, not cut, in 2025: from 1 September 2025 the universal industrial component raised interest co-financing by 2 percentage points and added grant components (highland, agrotourism, green, digitalisation, R&D) with larger grants for women; the RDA launched a consolidated Agro-Financing program in July 2026; the only identified cut is Anaklia's 2026 capital budget.

### Cited Findings
- From 1 Sep 2025 the universal industrial component of Produce in Georgia increases co-financing of loan and leasing interest by 2 percentage points — [1TV](https://1tv.ge/lang/en/news/enterprise-georgia-announces-changes-in-produce-in-georgia-program/); [Enterprise Georgia](https://www.enterprisegeorgia.gov.ge/en/News/produce-in-georgia-program-cha)
- New grant components: highland, agrotourism, green initiatives, digitalisation, R&D; higher grant amounts for women entrepreneurs; new eligible activities including food production, specialised construction work and additional annual crops — [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/georgia-expands-entrepreneur-support-programs-with-new-grants/); [BM.ge](https://bm.ge/en/news/produce-in-georgia-expands-support-programs-for-entrepreneurs)
- A technical-assistance component under the capital-market support programme is to be introduced in 2026 — [1TV](https://1tv.ge/lang/en/news/produce-in-georgia-chief-developing-capital-market-unlocks-new-opportunities-for-private-sector-to-access-finance/)
- Bank of Georgia and Basisbank list Produce in Georgia as a co-financed lending product — [BasisBank](https://bb.ge/en/business/business-financing/enterprise-georgia/atsarmoe-sakartveloshi)
- Overview of incentives to produce in Georgia (Estonian Chamber summary) — [Koda.ee](https://www.koda.ee/en/news/government-incentives-produce-georgia)
- RDA State Program of Agro-Financing (1 Jul 2026): up to 50% co-financing, max GEL 2 m; 55% for cooperatives/highland/dividing-line villages — [GeorgiaOnline](https://georgiaonline.ge/georgia-launches-state-agro-co-financing-program/)
- Anaklia 2026 funding cut from GEL 150 m to GEL 50 m — [Caucasus Watch](https://caucasuswatch.de/news/12865)

### Inferences
- For a founder, the practical stack is: Produce in Georgia interest co-financing on a bank/leasing loan (industrial or hotel/agro components) + RDA 50% capex co-financing for agri projects + FIZ tax exemptions for export-only manufacturing. Eligibility for foreign-owned Georgian LLCs should be confirmed program by program.
- Political-risk overlay: since late 2024 EU accession talks are frozen and Western donor programs (EU, USAID) have been curtailed; the retrieved sources did not quantify effects on Enterprise Georgia budgets.
