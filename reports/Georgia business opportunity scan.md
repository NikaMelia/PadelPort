# Ride Georgia's boom with software, not concrete

The best businesses for a software-capable founder in Georgia are asset-light products that sit on top of sectors growing faster than their tooling. Three stand out. First, turn PadelPort from a Georgian consumer app into a booking-and-operations platform for sports and leisure facilities, designed from day one for the Caucasus, Central Asia and the Gulf, because Georgia's roughly 40-60 padel courts cannot carry a standalone consumer app. Second, build Georgian vertical SaaS on the Revenue Service's rs.ge waybill and e-invoice APIs, where the notes found only one mature local accounting product and a Turkish entrant already arriving. Third, run software-enabled short-term-rental management in Batumi, where **17,052 apartments sold in 2025 (+17%)** into a market in which a typical unit grosses only about **USD 8,000 a year**. Behind these sit two capital-heavier but evidence-backed physical plays: an indoor padel club in Tbilisi, where a directory counts only **7 indoor courts against 33 outdoor**, and transit services in Kutaisi, where **1.8 million Wizz Air passengers** a year land at an airport with no hotel. The macro backdrop is unusually good: GDP grew **7.5% in 2025** and **6.9% in Q2 2026**, with ICT output up 16.6% and recreation up 17.4% year on year, wages rising about 10% a year, company registration at **GEL 100**, and a tax menu that includes a **1% turnover tax** for small businesses, **0% corporate tax on IT exports** and a new Innovative Startup status with **0% payroll tax for three years**. The avoid list is equally clear: Batumi residential towers (58,000 units in the pipeline), new Tbilisi Airbnb units (listings doubled to 12,195 in a year), generic Old Town restaurants (1,100 openings in 2024, covers down 20-25% in 2026), car re-exports, general-merchandise e-commerce against Temu, anything that depends on Russian relocants, and anything touching foreign grants or media under the 2025-26 foreign-influence laws. Political risk is high but has so far fallen on civil society rather than commerce; the more immediate operating risk is staffing, which **55% of surveyed companies** name as their top problem. One caveat governs everything below: the researchers could read only search-engine snippets, several headline figures conflict, and every capital estimate is the research team's inference rather than a sourced number.

## A 7.5%-growth economy that taxes small founders at 1%

Georgia has posted some of the fastest growth in wider Europe for four years running. Real GDP grew **9.7% in 2024** ([InternationalInvestment.biz citing Geostat](https://internationalinvestment.biz/en/business/6446-gdp-growth-in-georgia-for-2024-revised-up-to-97-new-geostat-estimate.html)) and **7.5% in 2025** on nominal output of about USD 38 billion ([Civil.ge](https://civil.ge/archives/726195); [IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/06/10/pr-26197-georgia-imf-executive-board-concludes-2026-article-iv-consultation)). The pace held into 2026: Q2 real GDP rose 6.9% year on year, and the fastest sectors were exactly the ones a software or recreation founder cares about, with **transportation and storage +19.8%, arts, entertainment and recreation +17.4%, and information and communication +16.6%** ([Xinhua citing Geostat](https://english.news.cn/20260919/2f11f2bdbf8d4822be477d41be8a6974/c.html)). Forecasters expect moderation rather than a stop: the IMF raised its 2026 forecast to 6.5% in June ([IMF](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/06/10/pr-26197-georgia-imf-executive-board-concludes-2026-article-iv-consultation)), the ADB to 6.3% in September ([1TV](https://1tv.ge/lang/en/news/adb-raises-georgias-2026-economic-growth-forecast-to-6-3/)), and the World Bank sits at 5% ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/world-bank-cuts-georgia-growth-forecast-to-5/)).

The consumer behind that growth is getting richer in nominal terms. The average monthly wage reached **GEL 2,466 in Q4 2025 (+11.2%)** and GEL 2,363.8 in Q1 2026 (+8.9%) ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/average-monthly-salaries-in-georgia-reach-gel-2466-in-q4-2025/); [Interpressnews](https://www.interpressnews.ge/en/article/148033-geostat-average-salary-in-georgia-reached-gel-23638-in-q1-2026/)), average household income rose 12.4% to GEL 2,131 a month ([1TV citing Geostat](https://1tv.ge/lang/en/news/geostat-average-monthly-household-income-in-georgia-rises-by-12-4/)), retail sales jumped **16.6% year on year in Q4 2025** ([Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/georgia/retail-sales-annual)), and unemployment fell to 13.3% in Q4 2025, the lowest since 2003 ([Trading Economics/Geostat](https://tradingeconomics.com/georgia/unemployment-rate)). The costs of that boom are visible too. Inflation re-accelerated to **5.6% in August 2026**, driven by fuel and food after the Iran war ([Civil.ge](https://civil.ge/archives/749811)), the National Bank raised its policy rate to 8.25% in May 2026 and has held it since ([NBG](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/media/news/the-national-bank-of-georgia-raises-the-monetary-policy-rate-by-0-25-percentage-points-to)), and lari bank lending averaged 13.9% even before the hike ([CEIC](https://www.ceicdata.com/en/indicator/georgia/bank-lending-rate)), with SME loans quoted at 12-16%+ ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/sme-bond-issuance-expands-funding-options-in-georgias-capital-market/)). The lari itself has been firm at around **2.60 per dollar in late September 2026** against a 2025 average of 2.74 ([Wise](https://wise.com/us/currency-converter/gel-to-usd-rate/history); [exchange-rates.org](https://www.exchange-rates.org/exchange-rate-history/usd-gel-2025)), which makes imported equipment (padel courts, servers, vehicles) cheaper but squeezes anyone earning in dollars and paying wages in lari. The market is also small and shrinking naturally: births hit a 32-year low in 2025 with a fertility rate of 1.61, and natural growth survives only in Tbilisi, Adjara and Kvemo Kartli ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/geostat-georgia-records-population-decline/)), while headline population is reported at either 3.94 million ([International Investment](https://internationalinvestment.biz/en/news/7862-foreigners-drive-the-main-population-growth-in-georgia-in-2025.html)) or 3.7 million ([Civil.ge](https://civil.ge/archives/677760)) depending on the source.

The tax and registration regime is where Georgia is genuinely exceptional. An LLC registers for **GEL 100 in one business day** at the House of Justice ([Georgia Expats](https://georgiaexpats.com/guides/llc-registration-georgia/)). An Individual Entrepreneur with Small Business Status pays **1% of turnover up to GEL 500,000 a year** (3% above), though consulting, legal, medical and financial-intermediation activities are excluded, which is a common trap ([Andersen Georgia](https://ge.andersen.com/1-tax-regime/); [Gegidze](https://www.gegidze.com/post/the-full-list-of-prohibited-activities-that-nullify-georgia-s-small-business-status)). February 2026 amendments tightened administration: status now runs from the application date, a missed monthly return is a fined offence even at zero income, and a cancelled status cannot be reinstated in the same year ([ExpatHub](https://expathub.ge/2026-updates-to-the-special-tax-regimes/)). Companies pay **15% corporate tax only on distributed profit** (the Estonian model), so reinvestment is tax-free ([Legal.ge](https://legal.ge/en/service/tax-law-en/georgia-corporate-income-tax-15-estonian-model)). Software exporters can take **Virtual Zone status for 0% corporate tax** on foreign IT income, provided the software is actually developed in Georgia ([Andersen VZP](https://ge.andersen.com/virtual-zone-person-georgia/); [Forbes Georgia](https://forbes.ge/en/virtual-zone-person-vs-international-it-company-in-georgia-2025-tax-guide-for-it-businesses/)), or International Company status at 5% ([Andersen IC](https://ge.andersen.com/international-company-status-georgia/)). Newest and most relevant for a product founder is the **Innovative Startup status in force since 24 September 2025**: 0% payroll income tax and 0% corporate tax in years one to three, stepping to 5% and then 10%, unlocked by either GEL 100,000 of private investment from a fund, angel network or accelerator, or a GEL 150,000 GITA grant ([ExpatHub](https://expathub.ge/georgia-startup-tax-benefits-2026/); [Andersen](https://ge.andersen.com/georgia-innovation-tax-incentives/)).

Public money has replaced the Western donor money that left. GITA's Startup Matching Grants run twice a year with **GEL 2 million per batch and up to GEL 150,000 per startup**, and a recent round gave 20 startups GEL 100,000 each ([GITA](https://gita.gov.ge/en/news/twenty-winner-startups-of-the-innovation-agency-s-matching-grants-program-4cIZDZpB8)). GITA's acceleration programme pays GEL 50,000 and GEL 150,000 at stages two and three, with Plug and Play and Startupbootcamp running cohorts from January 2026 and 500 Global and Founder Institute from July 2026 ([GITA](https://gita.gov.ge/en/programs/innovative-startup-acceleration-program-ELHEpPuv9)); the 500 Global Startup Academy for Eurasia runs July 2026 to June 2027 with grants up to GEL 150,000 ([500 Global](https://500.co/founders/eurasia/startup-academy)). For physical businesses, Enterprise Georgia's Produce in Georgia raised its interest co-financing on bank and leasing loans by two percentage points from 1 September 2025 and added grant windows for agrotourism, green, digitalisation and R&D projects ([1TV](https://1tv.ge/lang/en/news/enterprise-georgia-announces-changes-in-produce-in-georgia-program/); [Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/georgia-expands-entrepreneur-support-programs-with-new-grants/)), and the Rural Development Agency's Agro-Financing programme from 1 July 2026 co-finances up to **50% of a project to a GEL 2 million cap** ([GeorgiaOnline](https://georgiaonline.ge/georgia-launches-state-agro-co-financing-program/)). What has gone is USAID, cut by 95%+ and closed in July 2025 ([DevelopmentAid](https://www.developmentaid.org/news-stream/post/206934/what-the-collapse-of-usaid-has-cost-the-world)), and the EU's direct budget support ([Civil.ge](https://civil.ge/archives/627707)). Private foreign grants now need prior state approval with fines of double the grant if unapproved, and a March 2026 package criminalised unregistered foreign funding ([JAMnews](https://jam-news.net/what-new-georgian-law-on-grants-entails/); [HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/07/09/georgia-new-laws-devastate-independent-civic-groups)).

That last point frames the political risk honestly. EU accession is frozen, with Brussels calling Georgia a "candidate country in name only" in November 2025 ([OC Media](https://oc-media.org/eu-issues-2025-enlargement-report-says-georgia-is-a-candidate-country-in-name-only/)); the ruling party is asking the Constitutional Court to ban every parliamentary opposition party ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-16/georgia-s-ruling-party-moves-to-ban-all-parliamentary-opposition)); and the Commission has warned the whole population could lose Schengen visa-free travel ([European Commission](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_26_564)). Yet the DCFTA still operates ([EU DG Trade](https://policy.trade.ec.europa.eu/eu-trade-relationships-country-and-region/countries-and-regions/georgia_en)), no sanctions have hit the state or its banks, and the two banks that hold roughly 76-78% of assets follow US, EU and UK sanctions rules, which keeps correspondent access open ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/the-national-bank-of-georgia-and-the-western-sanctions/)). The practical lesson is that risk is acute for media, NGOs and anything grant-funded from abroad, and moderate for a purely commercial business, with the tail risks being visa-free loss, a secondary-sanctions incident in logistics, or a sharp Western response to a party ban. Two operational frictions matter more day to day. From 1 March 2026 any foreigner without permanent residence who works, freelances or actively runs a business in Georgia needs a work permit, enforced with inspections and GEL 1,000-7,000 fines and a transition to 1 January 2027 ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/new-work-permit-rules-georgia-immigration.html); [PB Services](https://pbservices.ge/blog/rules-for-foreign-nationals-working-in-georgia-2026/)), so if you are not a Georgian citizen this applies to you. And **55% of firms in the Business Association of Georgia's Q1 2026 index named the shortage of specialists as their top problem**, worst in construction and services ([Sova News](https://sovanews.tv/en/2026/05/01/bag-survey-specialist-shortage-remains-top-challenge-for-business/)), which, combined with 10%+ annual wage growth, means labour-light models have a structural edge.

The logic is that every documented Georgian startup success is B2B or infrastructure-adjacent rather than consumer: Pulsar AI sold to a US buyer in 2021 ([bne IntelliNews](https://www.intellinews.com/georgian-startup-pulsar-ai-taken-over-by-dealership-software-provider-spincar-210334/)), Payze went through Y Combinator and expanded across the CIS ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/471555-73)), Cargon raised USD 1.65 million from Kazakh, Azerbaijani and Flexport-linked investors ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/510785-56)), and Keepz signed 20,000 merchants in about two years. ## PadelPort's exit from a 40-court market is facility software sold regionally

Padel in Georgia is real but tiny. The national federation has been FIP-affiliated since 2022 ([Padel FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/federation/georgia/)), Tbilisi hosted an FIP Bronze tournament in 2025 with five match and four practice courts ([FIP](https://www.padelfip.com/events/fip-bronze-tbilisi-2025/)), and at least seven to nine operators are open in the capital, among them PadelHub in Saburtalo at **GEL 40 an hour before 3pm and GEL 60 after** ([PadelHub](https://www.padelhub.ge/)), Kus Tba with eight panoramic courts at Turtle Lake ([Kus Tba Padel](https://kustbapadel.ge/en/)), Padelbade claiming "1000+ active players" ([Padelbade](https://padelbade.com/)), two Padel Island sites ([Padel Island](https://www.padelisland.ge/)), and Padel Port in Batumi with four courts marketed by Radisson Blu to its guests ([Silk Hospitality](https://silkhospitality.com/radisson-blu-batumi/things-to-do/padel-port/)). A directory lists **18 Tbilisi entries totalling 7 indoor and 33 outdoor courts** ([MyPadelWay](https://mypadelway.com/ge/city/tbilisi)), and courts also exist in Kutaisi and Rustavi ([Padel Lands](https://padellands.com/en/pistas-de-padel/europa/georgia/)). Critically for PadelPort, a court-booking platform already exists: Padelio.ge operates online booking for Tbilisi ([Padelio](https://padelio.ge/)), and whether Playtomic is active in Georgia could not be established.

The arithmetic is unforgiving for a consumer booking app. Taking the notes' estimate of 40-60 courts nationally at GEL 40-60 an hour, even a generous six paid hours per court per day yields on the order of GEL 4-8 million (roughly USD 1.5-3 million) of total annual court hire in the whole country; a booking layer taking a few percent of that is a hobby, not a company. That is my own calculation from the sourced prices and court counts, not a figure from any source, and there is no Georgian utilisation or player-count data to refine it. Globally, though, padel is one of the fastest-growing sports on earth: **7,187 courts were added in 2024 (+26%) to reach 50,436, clubs rose 22% to 15,933, courts are expected to exceed 81,000 by 2027, and 92% of first-time players return** ([SGB Media on Playtomic](https://sgbonline.com/exec-study-predicts-continued-explosive-growth-for-padel-including-in-the-u-s/); [Padel Business Magazine](https://newsletter.padelbusinessmagazine.com/p/playtomic-report-global-padel-courts-top-50-000-up-17-in-2024)). The named high-growth markets include the UAE, the US, the UK, India and Indonesia ([Cluster Padel](https://www.clusterpadel.com/en/padel-news/20250630/a-new-era-of-global-expansion-over-50000-padel-courts-worldwide.aspx)). The opportunity, therefore, is to use Georgia as a cheap, tax-advantaged place to build and test club-side software (scheduling, dynamic pricing, coaching and league management, memberships, rs.ge-compliant invoicing, multi-gateway payments) and sell it to clubs in markets where courts are being built by the thousand, starting with the Caucasus and Central Asian markets where Georgian founders already have a track record and where FIP-affiliated federations are young.

Three features of the Georgian environment make this more attractive than it first appears. First, engineering is cheap and tax-light: the ICT sector's average wage was **GEL 4,373 a month in Q4 2025**, about USD 1,600 and roughly 1.8 times the national average ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/average-monthly-salaries-in-georgia-reach-gel-2466-in-q4-2025/)), and a Virtual Zone company pays no corporate tax on foreign software revenue as long as the code is written in Georgia ([Andersen VZP](https://ge.andersen.com/virtual-zone-person-georgia/)). Second, there is a funding ladder that did not exist five years ago: GITA grants of GEL 100,000-150,000, accelerator stage grants of GEL 50,000 and GEL 150,000, and then Innovative Startup status with zero payroll tax for three years once GEL 100,000 of qualifying investment or a GEL 150,000 GITA grant is in ([GITA](https://gita.gov.ge/en/programs/innovative-startup-acceleration-program-ELHEpPuv9); [ExpatHub](https://expathub.ge/georgia-startup-tax-benefits-2026/)). Regional seed money of USD 100,000-300,000 exists through Caucasus Ventures in Baku and INMerge ([Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/venture-capital/caucasus-ventures/__xYB8RSt9wRzbpU10zPdAhO2RX5gUZ-CTslfQoozkZiQ)), but Georgian-domiciled Series A capital essentially does not, and 500 Global's Georgia accelerator, which invested USD 5.9 million in 59 startups from a USD 20 million fund, concluded in June 2026 ([500 Global](https://500.co/content/launch-your-business-in-georgia-the-country-not-the-state)). Plan to raise abroad or re-domicile once you have traction, as every predecessor has. Third, the local plumbing is under-tooled in ways that favour a vertical product over a horizontal one. The Revenue Service exposes SOAP services for taxpayer data, electronic waybills and e-invoices ([GitHub rs-ge](https://github.com/leon-wbr/rs-ge)), waybills are mandatory for goods movement, and the only mature local integration found is the Balance.ge accounting suite ([Balance.ge](https://balance.ge/en/sruli-integracia-rs-gestan)). Merchants choose from more than a dozen payment gateways offering cards, BNPL, QR, open banking and crypto ([Quickpay](https://quickpay.ge/en/blog/georgian-payment-gateways-comparison)) with no clearing house behind them ([World Bank FSAP](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099840003012220978/pdf/P175014029297107b08e920ba260ea108d4.pdf)). A facility-management product that bundles booking, rs.ge compliance and payment reconciliation for padel clubs can be re-skinned for gyms (average Tbilisi membership GEL 147 a month ([Expatistan](https://www.expatistan.com/price/gym/tbilisi))), trampoline parks, tennis, ski rentals and clinics. The honest caveat is that SME software adoption rates were not retrievable, so the size of this domestic demand is inferred from the API surface and the arrival of a Turkish restaurant-SaaS competitor, not measured.

Two things to build in from day one. Keep more than 80% of revenue foreign so that Virtual Zone status holds and a domestic political shock does not strand the company, and keep the team remote-capable, because relocated engineers have proven mobile: one unverified agency estimate put IT departures at about 3,750 in 2025, mostly Russians, Ukrainians and Belarusians priced out by rent ([Voskhod](https://voskhod.agency/en/news/relocatsiyavit/)), even as a fresh, younger and poorer wave began arriving in late July 2026 on mobilisation fears ([Meduza](https://meduza.io/en/feature/2026/09/24/russians-are-leaving-the-country-again-amid-fears-of-another-mobilization-in-georgia-serbia-and-armenia-the-housing-rush-has-already-begun)). One source to disregard entirely: caucasusbusinessjournal.com's claims of USD 450 million of Q1 2025 venture funding and an "USD 85 million Series B for TbiliPay" match nothing else and appear fabricated.

## Indoor courts, Batumi keys and Kutaisi transfers are the physical gaps

If you want to own assets rather than software, the same padel evidence points to one specific gap: covered, climate-controlled courts. Seven of forty listed Tbilisi courts are indoor ([MyPadelWay](https://mypadelway.com/ge/city/tbilisi)), the city has hot summers and wet winters, and the successful Georgian consumer businesses of the last decade share a pattern of introducing a product new to the market and paying for an education phase, exactly as CoffeeLab did from 2016 and Shavi from 2021 before Shavi was named Best Coffee Shop at the 2025 European Coffee Trip Awards ([Perfect Daily Grind](https://perfectdailygrind.com/2026/01/georgia-specialty-coffee-market/)). The economics are tight, however. Court hire at GEL 40-60 an hour is USD 15-22, well under the EUR 25-40 typical in Europe, so a club lives on coaching, memberships, food and drink and corporate or tourist bookings rather than court time, and the notes' estimate is **USD 400,000 to 1 million for a four-to-six-court indoor conversion** including lease works, against 12-16% lari debt unless a Produce in Georgia subsidised loan applies. The Batumi hotel-partnered model, where Radisson Blu sells Padel Port's four courts to its guests ([Silk Hospitality](https://silkhospitality.com/radisson-blu-batumi/things-to-do/padel-port/)), is the lower-capital coastal variant and fits Batumi's 73% branded-hotel occupancy ([Eva Estate/Colliers](https://www.eva-estate.com/news/issue.asp?id=39)); if PadelPort the app is connected to that club, the natural move is to make the software the product and the club the reference customer. Nothing in the notes gives Georgian court utilisation, memberships or revenues, and no failed padel venue was found, which says more about how rarely Georgian failures get written up than about the risk.

Batumi's property market is the most quantified opportunity in the whole scan, and it points to services rather than ownership. **17,052 apartments sold in Batumi in 2025, up 17%, worth more than USD 1 billion** ([Georgia Today/Colliers](https://georgiatoday.ge/tbilisi-and-batumi-apartment-sales-surpass-4-3-billion-in-2025/)), while a typical Batumi short-term rental achieves 57% occupancy at an ADR of USD 43 and grosses about **USD 8,000 a year** ([Airbtics](https://airbtics.com/annual-airbnb-revenue-in-batumi-georgia/)). Galt & Taggart counts **58,000 new apartments in the Batumi pipeline for 2025-2029** with sellable stock rising ([Galt & Taggart](https://ramad.bog.ge/galt/Regional-Residential-Real-Estate_ENG_2025.pdf)), and analysts already expect weaker non-premium coastal prices ([Global Property Guide](https://www.globalpropertyguide.com/asia/georgia/price-history)). Thousands of absentee investors are about to receive keys to units that will struggle to earn, and the beneficiaries are the operators who pool, price, clean and market them. The researchers estimate a management company at USD 20,000-60,000 to start, and the software you would build for it (channel management, dynamic pricing, owner reporting) is itself a product. Tbilisi is the wrong city for the same idea because its listings have already doubled to 12,195 while long-term rents fell 12% ([AirROI](https://www.airroi.com/report/world/georgia/tbilisi/tbilisi); [BTU AI](https://btuai.ge/en/tbilisi-real-estate-market-prices-are-rising-rents-are-falling-who-wins-and-who-loses-in-the-new-reality/)); the Tbilisi service opportunity is converting under-performing Airbnbs back into mid-term furnished rentals.

Kutaisi is the clearest case of demand without supply. Wizz Air carried **1.8 million passengers through Kutaisi in 2025 on about 6,800 flights at a 99.7% load factor**, adding Lyon, Venice and Bratislava ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/wizz-air-carries-1-8-million-passengers-from-kutaisi-in-2025/)), over a quarter of Georgia's 5.5 million tourists arrived through it ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/travel/2026/01/31/natural-wine-long-hikes-and-history-could-kutaisi-be-georgias-next-great-travel-destinatio)), yet there is no airport hotel ([Sleeping in Airports](https://www.sleepinginairports.net/guides/kutaisi-airport-guide.htm)), regional hotel occupancy sits around 35%, and an academic review calls Imereti's roads and hotels below international standard ([Georgian Scientists](https://journals.4science.ge/index.php/GS/article/view/3234)). Almost every passenger is a budget European transiting to Tbilisi, Batumi or the mountains. Pre-booked fixed-price transfers, early-flight lodging, luggage storage and one-to-two-day experience products address that flow, and they also address the single most repeated tourist complaint in the notes, airport taxi overcharging, where one visitor paid GEL 280 for a ride that costs about GEL 25 on Bolt ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.co.uk/ShowTopic-g294195-i9344-k14920782-Scammed_taxi_or_Illegal_taxi_driver_report-Tbilisi.html)). The evidence for the complaint is anecdotal and the business is operationally heavy, but the capital estimate of USD 30,000-100,000 is the lowest of any physical play here.

Tourism as a whole is worth serving only where the mix is shifting toward you. Headline 2025 numbers were records: **5.52 million tourist visits (+8.4%)** ([1TV](https://1tv.ge/lang/en/news/national-tourism-administration-georgia-welcomes-record-5-5-million-tourists-in-2025/)) and **USD 4.69 billion in revenue (+6%)** ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/georgias-tourism-revenue-tops-usd-4-69-billion-in-2025/)). But hoteliers called the record "humiliating" and said Tbilisi room prices had not been so low in 30 years ([Voxeurop](https://voxeurop.eu/en/georgia-businesses-crisis-russia/)), Tbilisi branded-hotel occupancy was 58% in Q4 2025 at an ADR of USD 87 ([Galt & Taggart](https://galtandtaggart.com/report/tourism-market-watch-4q25/)), Q1 2026 visits were flat with Israel down 17.5%, Iran down 48.8% and India down 29.5% while the EU rose 30.2%, Saudi Arabia 36.6% and China 48.6% ([Civil.ge](https://civil.ge/archives/732723)), and Tbilisi restaurants reported 20-25% fewer covers ([OC Media](https://oc-media.org/how-the-iran-war-is-putting-a-dent-in-georgias-tourism-industry/)). The growth segments are Gulf families who stay for months and are already served by Arab restaurants but rarely by halal-certified Georgian cuisine ([Armenpress](https://armenpress.am/en/article/1127707)), EU budget flyers via Kutaisi, and Chinese groups. Dental tourism is proven but crowded, with packages of USD 2,900-9,660 and clinics claiming thousands of foreign patients a year ([Medical Tourism Co](https://www.medicaltourismco.com/clinics/dentist/georgia/); [Bookimed](https://health.bookimed.com/clinics/country=georgia/city=tbilisi/)), so the gap is demand generation and packaging rather than another clinic. Cloud kitchens exploiting the 25-30% commissions charged by Wolt, Glovo and Bolt Food are plausible at USD 30,000-80,000 per kitchen, but the only source for the commission figures and for GeraEats's 15-18% alternative is GeraEats itself ([GeraEats](https://geraeats.com/blog/geraeats-in-georgia-2026)).

For a founder with more capital or agricultural partners, the export and logistics niches are real but slow and outside a software skill set. Blueberries are the strongest agri story, with production above 7,500 tonnes and 95% exported in 2025 ([FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/north-america/article/9814617/georgia-s-blueberry-exports-expand-as-sector-targets-eu-growth-and-new-markets/)), but they need USD 1-3 million and three years, and foreigners cannot own farmland ([Legal.ge](https://legal.ge/en/service/civil-law-en/agricultural-land-transactions-en)). Hazelnuts earned USD 171.7 million in 2025 with prices up 39% to USD 8.46/kg ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/hazelnut-export-value-rises-despite-drop-in-volume-ministry/); [MEPA](https://mepa.gov.ge/En/News/Details/24142)), so the play is kernel processing and traceability, not orchards. Wine exported USD 267.9 million in 2025 at a rising USD 2.98 a litre ([Wine Intelligence](https://wine-intelligence.com/blogs/wine-analytics-pricing-report-data/georgia-reports-strong-wine-and-spirits-export-performance-in-2025)), but 62.6% still went to Russia in January-August 2025 ([Commersant](https://commersant.ge/en/news/business/georgian-wine-exports-down-by-about-16-the-main-export-market-is-russia)), so the room for a new entrant is premium qvevri wine brokered to the US (15.5% CAGR 2021-24 ([Wine Industry Advisor](https://wineindustryadvisor.com/2025/12/09/growing-2025-exports-to-the-u-s-underpinning-georgias-strong-sales-momentum/))), Japan and France, at thin margins. Logistics is capacity-constrained rather than demand-constrained: APM Terminals Poti handled a record 600,000+ TEU in 2025 and is at capacity ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/apm-terminals-poti-reports-significant-growth-in-container-and-cargo-volumes/)), Middle Corridor transit doubled since 2018 to about 4.6-5 million tonnes ([PMCG](https://pmcg-i.com/research/middle-corridor-transit-cargo-flows-georgia/)), and Anaklia's Chinese partner has exited with operations not expected before 2029 ([Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/china-quietly-exits-georgian-port-deal); [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/russia-eurasia/politika/2026/04/middle-corridor-transport-prospect)), which favours off-dock yards and bonded warehousing at USD 0.5-5 million. In energy, EV imports rose 151% to 5,402 in 2024 ([Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/as-georgias-electric-vehicle-market-grows-china-powers-ahead)) and Tbilisi's charger auction bids jumped 11.5 times ([Georgia Today](https://georgiatoday.ge/tbilisi-installs-130-fast-ev-chargers-after-auction-bids-jump-11-5x/)), so urban charging is already contested and the remaining niche is intercity and destination charging at wineries and hotels; rooftop solar under updated net-metering rules is the lowest-capital energy entry, but the capacity cap and credit terms could not be verified ([EBRD GEFF](https://ebrdgeff.com/georgia/en/english-georgia-updates-net-metering-rules-for-microgeneration/)).

## Data limitations: snippets, conflicting figures and one suspect source

Every finding in this report rests on search-engine snippets of the cited pages, not on full reads. The research environment blocked direct fetches of nearly every primary source (Geostat, the National Bank, GNTA, Galt & Taggart, GITA, Civil.ge, Georgia Today, the padel club sites and directories, TripAdvisor, Reddit), and each researcher's search quota ran out before every question was covered. Several load-bearing figures conflict across sources and could not be reconciled: total 2025 international visits are reported as 7.8 million, "nearly 7 million" and 8.2 million; ICT exports appear as USD 209 million for 2023 and USD 842 million for 2024 despite a reported 9.2% decline, which can only be a definitional difference; 2025 wine exports are USD 267.9 million on the National Wine Agency basis but far lower on a Geostat-based series; Batumi has either 3,205 or 9,258 active short-term rentals depending on the data vendor; population is 3.94 or 3.7 million; and record tourism revenue coexists with hoteliers reporting 30-year-low prices. The padel court count comes from a single directory and may include stale or duplicate listings. The 2025 IT-departure figure (3,750) comes from one relocation agency and is unverified. Every capital estimate and time-to-revenue figure is the research team's inference from international norms and listings, not a Georgian benchmark. Whole categories the brief asked about returned no data at all: SME software adoption rates, padel utilisation and player numbers, medical-tourism volumes after 2017, kids' services, elderly care, language schools, Batumi- or Kutaisi-specific resident complaints, and named startup post-mortems. One outlet, caucasusbusinessjournal.com, shows strong signs of fabrication and has been excluded from every figure above. Before committing capital to any idea here, verify the load-bearing number against the linked primary source.

## Conclusion

The pattern across five research streams is that Georgia's growth is outrunning its tooling, and that the businesses which have survived here, from Payze and Keepz to Balance.ge and the Kutaisi transfer trade, sell picks and shovels to that growth rather than betting on the consumer directly. For a founder holding a padel app, that reframes the question: PadelPort's value is not the Georgian padel player, of whom there are too few, but the operational knowledge of running courts in a fast-growing sport, which can be productised, exported under Virtual Zone status and funded through a grant ladder that the state has been expanding even as Western donors left. The same logic makes Batumi's coming glut of investor-owned apartments a management-software opportunity rather than a real-estate one, and Kutaisi's 1.8 million budget passengers a logistics product rather than a hotel.

The implication going forward is to sequence deliberately: start with the asset-light software plays that can be launched for under USD 150,000 and a GITA grant, use them to earn foreign revenue and Innovative Startup status, and only then consider the indoor club or the Batumi operation, ideally with the software as the differentiator and a partner carrying the lease. Keep revenue mostly foreign, the team remote-capable and every plan free of Anaklia, Russian demand and foreign grant money, and the political risk that dominates the headlines becomes a manageable tail rather than a business-ending one.
